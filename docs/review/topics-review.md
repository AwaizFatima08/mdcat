# Important topics — review sheet

Every unit as ranked in the app (our estimate, not official PM&DC weightage). Mark anything you would move or reword next to its unit id.

## Biology

### Must-know

**bio-03 — 3. Biological Molecules**

The largest Biology unit by LO count: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, water and nucleic acids. Its structures and bonds come back in enzymes, digestion and inheritance.

- BIO-3.5: Describe amino acids and the structure of proteins.
- BIO-3.4: Discuss monosaccharides, oligosaccharides and polysaccharides.
- BIO-3.6: Describe phospholipids, triglycerides, alcohols and esters (acylglycerols).

**bio-15 — 15. Homeostasis**

Eight LOs on the kidney and thermoregulation. Nephron steps (filtration, reabsorption, secretion) and the two capillary beds are classic MCQ material.

- BIO-15.2: Explain glomerular filtration, selective reabsorption and tubular secretion.
- BIO-15.1: Explain organs of the urinary system; relate kidney structure to function.
- BIO-15.4: Compare glomerular and peritubular capillaries.

**bio-10 — 10. Inheritance**

Seven LOs and a favourite for problem-style MCQs: Mendelian ratios, linkage and crossing over, and sex-linked traits such as hemophilia.

- BIO-10.2: Explain the law of independent assortment with an example.
- BIO-10.6: Describe inheritance of sex-linked traits.
- BIO-10.3: Describe gene linkage and crossing over.

**bio-05 — 5. Coordination & Control**

Seven LOs covering neurons, nerve impulses, reflex arcs and brain parts. Matching brain regions to their functions is a common question type.

- BIO-5.7: Describe the functions of each part.
- BIO-5.2: Explain the structure of a typical neuron.
- BIO-5.5: Explain the functions of the components of a reflex arc.

**bio-09 — 9. Support & Movement**

Seven LOs on cartilage, bone, muscle and joints. Sarcomere structure and the sliding filament steps of contraction are detail-heavy and frequently tested.

- BIO-9.5: Describe the process of skeletal muscle contraction.
- BIO-9.4: Explain the ultrastructure of skeletal muscle.
- BIO-9.3: Compare smooth, cardiac and skeletal muscle.

### Important

**bio-06 — 6. Enzymes**

Only four LOs, but enzyme action, the effects of temperature and pH, and competitive versus non-competitive inhibition are asked regularly and link to Biological Molecules.

- BIO-6.4: Describe enzyme inhibitors.
- BIO-6.3: Describe effects of temperature, pH and concentration on enzyme action.
- BIO-6.2: Explain the mechanism of action of enzymes.

**bio-12 — 12. Immunity**

One LO, but it spans a whole chapter: B and T cells, antibodies, active and passive immunity. Worth more time than its LO count suggests.

- BIO-12.1: Define and discuss the functions and importance of specific defence mechanisms.

**bio-11 — 11. Circulation**

Heart structure, phases of the heartbeat, vessels and the lymphatic system. Facts are concrete and easy to test as direct MCQs.

- BIO-11.2: Describe the phases of heartbeat.
- BIO-11.1: Discuss the general structure of the human heart.
- BIO-11.3: List differences and functions of arteries, veins and capillaries.

**bio-04 — 4. Cell Structure & Function**

Organelle structure and function, prokaryote versus eukaryote, and chromosomes. Basic cell knowledge that many other Biology units assume.

- BIO-4.3: Outline structure and function of nucleus, ER, Golgi apparatus and mitochondria.
- BIO-4.2: Compare and contrast prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.
- BIO-4.4: Describe the structure, chemical composition and function of chromosomes.

**bio-13 — 13. Respiration**

Three LOs on the respiratory tract, gas exchange in the alveoli and the effects of smoking. Short, concrete and regularly asked.

- BIO-13.2: Discuss gas exchange in human lungs.
- BIO-13.1: Discuss the functions of the main parts of the respiratory system.
- BIO-13.3: Discuss the effect of smoking on the respiratory system.

**bio-16 — 16. Biotechnology**

Vaccines, DNA probes, monoclonal antibodies and products such as insulin. Applied, medically relevant facts that suit straightforward MCQs.

- BIO-16.2: State the role of biotechnology in diagnosis (DNA/RNA probes, monoclonal antibodies).
- BIO-16.1: Describe how biotechnologists produce vaccines.
- BIO-16.3: Describe products obtained for disease treatment.

### Quick wins

**bio-08 — 8. Reproduction**

Three LOs: reproductive organs, hormones of the menstrual cycle and common STDs. A hormone timeline learnt once gives quick marks.

- BIO-8.2: Describe the menstrual cycle emphasizing the role of hormones.
- BIO-8.1: Describe the male and female reproductive systems and their regulating hormones.
- BIO-8.3: List common STDs with causative agents and main symptoms.

**bio-07 — 7. Evolution**

Three LOs comparing Lamarck and Darwin plus origin of life. Mostly recall of theory points, so fast to learn.

- BIO-7.3: Explain Darwin's theory of natural selection.
- BIO-7.2: Describe Lamarck's inheritance of acquired characters.
- BIO-7.1: Explain origin of life according to the concept of evolution.

**bio-01 — 1. Acellular Life**

Two LOs: virus classification and HIV/AIDS transmission and symptoms. A small, factual unit that is quick to revise.

- BIO-1.1: Classify viruses on basis of their structure / number of strands / diseases / hosts.
- BIO-1.2: Identify symptoms, mode of transmission and cause of viral disease (AIDS).

**bio-14 — 14. Digestion**

Two LOs on the parts of the gut and its glands. Enzymes and secretions of each region are easy recall marks.

- BIO-14.2: Explain functions of the main parts including associated structures and glands.
- BIO-14.1: Describe the parts of the human digestive system.

**bio-02 — 2. Bioenergetics**

A single LO on how proteins and fats enter respiration compared with glucose. Learn the entry points into glycolysis and the Krebs cycle.

- BIO-2.1: Outline the cellular respiration of proteins and fats and correlate these with that of glucose.

## Chemistry

### Must-know

**chem-14 — 14. Chemistry of Hydrocarbons**

The biggest Chemistry unit with 19 LOs: alkanes, alkenes, alkynes and benzene. Reaction types and benzene substitution underpin all later organic units.

- CHEM-14.12: Describe the mechanism of electrophilic substitution in benzene.
- CHEM-14.14: Apply the directing effects of substituents in electrophilic substitution of benzene.
- CHEM-14.6: Describe the structure and reactivity of alkenes (ethene).

**chem-10 — 10. Chemical Bonding**

VSEPR shapes, hybridisation, sigma and pi bonds and polarity. Bonding ideas are needed across organic and inorganic chemistry.

- CHEM-10.1: Use VSEPR theory to describe the shapes of molecules.
- CHEM-10.3: Describe the shapes of simple molecules using orbital hybridisation.
- CHEM-10.5: Predict molecular polarity from molecular shape.

**chem-06 — 6. Chemical Equilibrium**

Le Chatelier shifts, Ksp, common ion effect and buffers. Mostly concept MCQs, plus simple solubility product problems; links to kinetics and thermochemistry.

- CHEM-6.3: State Le Chatelier's principle and apply it to changes in concentration, pressure, temperature or catalyst.
- CHEM-6.4: Define and explain the solubility product.
- CHEM-6.6: Define buffer solutions and explain their types.

**chem-02 — 2. Atomic Structure**

Eight LOs on orbitals, quantum numbers and electronic configuration. Aufbau, Pauli and Hund's rule questions are quick to set and common.

- CHEM-2.7: Use the Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle and Hund's rule to write electronic configurations.
- CHEM-2.4: Distinguish among principal energy level, energy sub-level and atomic orbital.
- CHEM-2.8: Write the electronic configuration of atoms.

**chem-03 — 3. Gases**

Nine LOs on gas laws, the ideal gas equation and real gases. Numericals with PV = nRT and units of R are standard.

- CHEM-3.7: Derive the ideal gas equation using Boyle's, Charles's and Avogadro's laws.
- CHEM-3.8: Explain the significance and different units of the gas constant R.
- CHEM-3.9: Distinguish between real and ideal gases.

**chem-07 — 7. Reaction Kinetics**

Rate laws, order of reaction and activation energy. Seven LOs that mix concept questions with short calculations.

- CHEM-7.4: Give the order with respect to a reactant and write the rate law.
- CHEM-7.5: Explain activation energy and activated complex.
- CHEM-7.3: Explain qualitatively the factors affecting the rate of reaction.

**chem-01 — 1. Fundamental Concepts**

Mole ratios, limiting reagent and percentage yield. Stoichiometry numericals are fast marks once the method is practised.

- CHEM-1.2: Perform stoichiometric calculations with balanced equations using moles, representative particles, masses and volumes of gases (at STP).
- CHEM-1.4: Calculate the maximum amount of product and the amount of any unreacted excess reagent.
- CHEM-1.5: Given any two of theoretical yield, actual yield and percentage yield, calculate the third.

### Important

**chem-08 — 8. Thermochemistry & Energetics**

Seven LOs on enthalpy, the first law and Hess's law. Energy cycles and definitions of state functions are regularly asked.

- CHEM-8.6: Apply Hess's law to construct simple energy cycles.
- CHEM-8.3: Define system, surroundings, boundary, state function, heat, heat capacity, internal energy, work and enthalpy.
- CHEM-8.5: Explain the first law of thermodynamics.

**chem-16 — 16. Alcohols & Phenols**

Six LOs on alcohols and phenols: reactivity, esters and ethers, and telling an alcohol from a phenol. Builds directly on hydrocarbons.

- CHEM-16.2: Explain the reactivity of alcohols.
- CHEM-16.6: Distinguish between an alcohol and a phenol.
- CHEM-16.4: Explain the nomenclature, structure and reactivity of phenol.

**chem-17 — 17. Aldehydes & Ketones**

Six LOs on aldehydes and ketones. Nucleophilic addition and oxidation tests that distinguish the two are common reaction MCQs.

- CHEM-17.4: Describe acid- and base-catalysed nucleophilic addition reactions.
- CHEM-17.6: Describe oxidation reactions of aldehydes and ketones.
- CHEM-17.3: Describe and compare the reactivity of aldehydes and ketones.

**chem-15 — 15. Alkyl Halides**

SN1, SN2, E1 and E2 mechanisms in four LOs. Mechanism comparison questions are popular and link alkanes to alcohols.

- CHEM-15.3: Describe the mechanisms and types of nucleophilic substitution (SN1, SN2).
- CHEM-15.4: Describe the mechanisms and types of elimination (E1, E2).
- CHEM-15.2: Discuss the structure and reactivity of RX.

**chem-09 — 9. Electrochemistry**

Oxidation numbers, redox and electrode potentials. Assigning oxidation numbers is quick to test and used throughout chemistry.

- CHEM-9.3: Use oxidation-number change to identify atoms oxidised or reduced.
- CHEM-9.5: Define the standard electrode potential of an electrode.
- CHEM-9.4: Define cathode, anode, electrode potential and the standard hydrogen electrode.

**chem-11 — 11. s- and p-Block Elements**

Periodic trends in radius, ionisation energy and electronegativity, plus Group I, II and IV reactions. Trend questions are common and quick.

- CHEM-11.1: Define and explain atomic, ionic and covalent radii, ionisation energy, electron affinity, electronegativity, bond energy and bond length.
- CHEM-11.3: Describe reactions of Group I elements with water, oxygen and chlorine.
- CHEM-11.4: Describe reactions of Group II elements with water, oxygen and chlorine.

**chem-05 — 5. Solids**

Five LOs on crystal types, lattices and lattice energy. Comparing ionic and molecular crystals is straightforward recall.

- CHEM-5.3: Describe ionic and molecular crystals.
- CHEM-5.5: Define lattice energy.
- CHEM-5.1: Describe crystalline solids.

### Quick wins

**chem-04 — 4. Liquids**

Four LOs: vapour pressure, boiling point, hydrogen bonding and why water is densest at 4 °C. Short and easy to score.

- CHEM-4.3: Describe hydrogen bonding in H₂O, NH₃ and HF.
- CHEM-4.2: Explain evaporation, vapour pressure and boiling point.
- CHEM-4.4: Explain the anomalous behaviour of water (maximum density at 4 °C).

**chem-13 — 13. Fundamental Principles of Organic Chemistry**

Four LOs on classification, functional groups and stereoisomerism. A small unit that supports every organic chapter.

- CHEM-13.4: Explain stereoisomerism and its types.
- CHEM-13.3: Define functional group.
- CHEM-13.2: Classify organic compounds on a structural basis.

**chem-18 — 18. Carboxylic Acids**

Three LOs on carboxylic acids and their derivatives. Learn the conversions to acyl halides, anhydrides, esters and amides as one chart.

- CHEM-18.3: Describe conversion of carboxylic acids to acyl halides, anhydrides, esters and amides, and their interconversion.
- CHEM-18.2: Discuss the reactivity of carboxylic acids.
- CHEM-18.1: Describe the nomenclature, structure and preparation of carboxylic acids.

**chem-19 — 19. Macromolecules**

Three LOs on proteins and enzymes, overlapping heavily with Biology. Revising one subject covers much of the other.

- CHEM-19.1: Explain the classification and structure–function relationship of proteins.
- CHEM-19.3: Describe the role of enzymes as biocatalysts.
- CHEM-19.2: Describe the roles of proteins in body functions and their nutritional importance.

**chem-20 — 20. Industrial Chemistry**

Three recall LOs on adhesives, dyes and polymers. Addition versus condensation polymers is the most useful distinction.

- CHEM-20.3: Know about addition and condensation polymers and their sub-types.
- CHEM-20.2: Know the types of dyes and their uses.
- CHEM-20.1: Know the types and applications of adhesives.

**chem-12 — 12. Transition Elements**

A single LO on d-block electronic configurations. Remember the chromium and copper exceptions and how ions lose 4s electrons first.

- CHEM-12.1: Describe the electronic structures of d-block elements and their ions.

## Physics

### Must-know

**phy-06 — 6. Waves**

The largest Physics unit with 18 LOs: wave terms, speed of sound, stationary waves, organ pipes and SHM. Many short numericals with v = fλ.

- PHY-6.15: Describe stationary waves in vibrating air columns (organ pipes).
- PHY-6.4: Solve problems using v = fλ.
- PHY-6.17: Explain simple harmonic motion and its characteristics.

**phy-02 — 2. Force & Motion**

Seventeen LOs on kinematics, projectiles, Newton's laws and momentum. Projectile range and collision problems are favourite numericals.

- PHY-2.11: Use the equations of motion to find a projectile's maximum height, range, position at a given time and time of flight; the launch angle for maximum range and angles giving equal ranges.
- PHY-2.15: Solve problems on elastic and inelastic collisions in one dimension using conservation of momentum.
- PHY-2.13: Describe Newton's second law as the rate of change of momentum.

**phy-05 — 5. Fluid Dynamics**

Ten LOs on fluid flow. The continuity equation and Bernoulli's effect, including blood flow, give both concept and numerical questions.

- PHY-5.6: Describe the equation of continuity Av = constant and solve problems with it.
- PHY-5.8: Interpret and apply Bernoulli's effect, including in blood flow.
- PHY-5.1: Describe the terminal velocity of an object.

**phy-08 — 8. Electrostatics**

Nine LOs on Coulomb's law, electric field, potential and capacitor charging and discharging. Field and potential calculations for point charges are standard.

- PHY-8.1: State Coulomb's law and explain the reduced force in a medium.
- PHY-8.3: Calculate the electric field at a point due to two charges of the same or opposite signs.
- PHY-8.6: Define electric potential at a point as work done per unit positive charge from infinity.

**phy-03 — 3. Work & Energy**

Eight LOs on work, kinetic and potential energy, power and efficiency. Formula-based questions that link closely to Force & Motion.

- PHY-3.1: Describe work as the product of force and displacement in the direction of the force.
- PHY-3.6: Express power as the scalar product of force and velocity.
- PHY-3.8: State the implications of energy losses in practical devices; efficiency.

### Important

**phy-07 — 7. Thermodynamics**

First law of thermodynamics, work in a volume change and Cp − Cv = R. Seven LOs that overlap with Chemistry thermochemistry.

- PHY-7.4: Describe the first law in terms of change in internal energy, heat supplied and work done.
- PHY-7.7: Apply the first law to derive Cp − Cv = R for an ideal gas.
- PHY-7.3: Calculate work done by a thermodynamic system during a volume change.

**phy-09 — 9. Current Electricity**

Ohm's law, resistivity, internal resistance and maximum power transfer. Short numericals on circuits that are quick once the formulas are clear.

- PHY-9.4: Explain the internal resistance of sources and its consequences.
- PHY-9.5: Describe the condition for maximum power transfer.
- PHY-9.3: Define resistivity and explain its dependence on temperature.

**phy-11 — 11. Electromagnetic Induction**

Faraday's and Lenz's laws plus transformers. Turns-ratio problems and predicting the direction of induced current are common.

- PHY-11.2: Use Lenz's law to predict the direction of induced current and relate it to conservation of energy.
- PHY-11.3: Describe the construction and working of a transformer.
- PHY-11.1: State Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction.

**phy-16 — 16. Nuclear Physics**

Nuclear decay, half-life with λ = 0.693/T½, and medical uses of radiation. Half-life numericals are quick marks.

- PHY-16.3: Describe half-life and solve problems using λ = 0.693/T½.
- PHY-16.2: Identify the spontaneous and random nature of nuclear decay.
- PHY-16.4: Describe the biological effects of radiation and its medical uses.

**phy-10 — 10. Electromagnetism**

Magnetic flux and the force on a moving charge in a magnetic field. The circular path of a charge is a classic short question.

- PHY-10.4: Explain the force on a charged particle in a uniform magnetic field.
- PHY-10.3: Describe the path of a charged particle shot perpendicular into a magnetic field.
- PHY-10.2: Describe magnetic flux as the scalar product of B and A.

### Quick wins

**phy-04 — 4. Rotational & Circular Motion**

Four LOs on radians and the links between linear and angular quantities, v = rω and a = rα. Small and fast to learn, and used in circular motion and SHM.

- PHY-4.4: Relate linear and angular displacement, velocity and acceleration.
- PHY-4.1: Define angular displacement and express it in radians.
- PHY-4.3: Describe angular velocity.

**phy-01 — 1. Vectors & Equilibrium**

Three LOs on vector components and dot and cross products. Quick to learn and needed for work, power and magnetic force.

- PHY-1.1: Determine the sum of vectors using perpendicular (rectangular) components.
- PHY-1.2: Describe the scalar product of two vectors in terms of the angle between them.
- PHY-1.3: Describe the vector product of two vectors in terms of the angle between them.

**phy-12 — 12. Alternating Current**

Phase in AC circuits through R, L and C, plus the order of the electromagnetic spectrum. Mostly recall, so a fast win.

- PHY-12.2: Explain AC through resistors, capacitors and inductors.
- PHY-12.3: Describe the electromagnetic spectrum from radio waves to gamma rays.
- PHY-12.1: Describe the phase of AC and how phase lag and lead occur.

**phy-13 — 13. Electronics**

Two LOs on the PN junction, biasing and rectification. Learn half-wave versus full-wave rectifiers for easy marks.

- PHY-13.2: Describe the PN junction and its forward and reverse biasing.
- PHY-13.1: Define rectification and describe diodes in half- and full-wave rectification.

**phy-14 — 14. Dawn of Modern Physics**

One LO on photons with energy E = hf. A single idea that also supports atomic spectra and chemistry's atomic structure.

- PHY-14.1: Explain the particle model of light in terms of photons with energy.

**phy-15 — 15. Atomic Spectra**

One LO on line spectra, produced when electrons move between energy levels. Small and quick, and it links to hydrogen atom theory in Chemistry.

- PHY-15.1: Describe and explain atomic (line) spectra.

## English

### Must-know

**eng-02 — 2. Formal & Lexical Aspects of Language**

Two-thirds of the English LOs: tenses, prepositions, voice, narration, punctuation and vocabulary in context. If the paper follows the LO share, this unit carries most of the English marks.

- ENG-2.4: Use tenses correctly.
- ENG-2.8: Use prepositions of position, time, movement and direction.
- ENG-2.13: Use active and passive voice appropriately.

### Important

**eng-03 — 3. Writing Skills**

Error spotting and sentence correction, especially subject–verb agreement. Practice with grammar rules from the lexical unit carries over directly.

- ENG-3.3: Apply subject–verb agreement.
- ENG-3.2: Correct faulty sentence structure.
- ENG-3.1: Proofread and edit text for errors of usage and style.

### Quick wins

**eng-01 — 1. Reading & Thinking Skills**

Three LOs on reading a passage and working out meaning from context. Careful reading rather than memorising earns these marks.

- ENG-1.2: Deduce meanings from the context.
- ENG-1.1: Scan a passage to answer short questions.
- ENG-1.3: Analyse how a writer or poet uses language that appeals to the senses (figurative language).

## Logical Reasoning

### Must-know

**lr-02 — 2. Letter & Symbol Series**

Number, letter and symbol series follow fixed patterns such as arithmetic and geometric steps. Practice makes these some of the fastest marks in the paper.

- LR-2.1: Apply arithmetic operations to number series.
- LR-2.3: Complete letter and symbol series by specific rules.
- LR-2.2: Apply geometric progressions to number series.

**lr-01 — 1. Critical Thinking**

Judging whether statements are true or false and spotting false beliefs. The core skill behind most other reasoning question types.

- LR-1.1: Develop logical arguments for statements to be true or false.
- LR-1.3: Identify and evaluate false beliefs using logical reasoning.
- LR-1.2: Give reasons for right beliefs.

### Important

**lr-06 — 6. Cause & Effect**

Three LOs on linking events to their causes and rejecting false explanations. Question patterns repeat, so practice pays off.

- LR-6.1: Give reasons for incidents, events and accidents.
- LR-6.2: Reject false beliefs through valid arguments.
- LR-6.3: Build positive thinking through strong arguments.

**lr-05 — 5. Course of Action**

Three LOs on picking the most sensible action from given facts. Read every option against the information given, not personal opinion.

- LR-5.3: Judge courses of action using arguments.
- LR-5.2: Use information to make decisions.
- LR-5.1: Gather the different parts of the information given.

### Quick wins

**lr-03 — 3. Logical Deductions**

Two LOs on drawing new relations from given ones, such as orders and arrangements. Practise a few patterns for quick marks.

- LR-3.1: Predict new relations from given relations.
- LR-3.2: Build new structures from information in given structures.

**lr-04 — 4. Logical Problems**

Two LOs on deductive puzzles where one result unlocks the next. A grid or diagram makes them quick to solve.

- LR-4.2: Solve puzzles by deductive reasoning.
- LR-4.1: Use the result of one problem to resolve another.
