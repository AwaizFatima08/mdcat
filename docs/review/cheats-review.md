## Biology (50 cards)

### 1. Acellular Life

**bio-01-c01 — Classifying viruses: genome and shape** _(table)_

A virus has DNA or RNA, never both. Capsid = protein coat made of capsomeres.

| Genome | Examples |
|---|---|
| dsDNA | Pox (smallpox), herpes, adenovirus, hepatitis B, phage T4 |
| ssDNA | Parvovirus, phage φX174 |
| ssRNA | TMV, polio, influenza, measles, rabies, HIV |
| dsRNA | Reovirus (rotavirus) |

| Shape | Example |
|---|---|
| Helical (rod) | TMV |
| Polyhedral | Adenovirus, polio |
| Complex (head + tail) | Bacteriophage T4 |

💡 Most plant viruses are RNA viruses. Envelope (influenza, HIV, herpes) comes from the host cell membrane.

⚠️ T4 tail lysozyme digests the bacterial wall; only DNA enters. Lysogenic phage DNA in the host chromosome = prophage.


**bio-01-c02 — HIV: how it takes over a helper T cell** _(map)_

- gp120 on the envelope binds the CD4 receptor of a helper T cell (also macrophages)
- → envelope fuses; RNA core enters
- → reverse transcriptase: viral RNA → DNA (hence 'retrovirus')
- → integrase inserts it into host DNA = provirus
- → host makes new viral RNA and proteins
- → new virions bud off, taking host membrane

Genome: two copies of ssRNA. Falling CD4 count tracks progress to AIDS; patients die of opportunistic infections (e.g. Pneumocystis pneumonia) and cancers such as Kaposi's sarcoma.

💡 Spread: blood, semen, vaginal fluid, breast milk, shared needles, mother to baby. NOT by mosquitoes, touch, sharing food or coughing.

⚠️ Window period: a newly infected person may test antibody-negative. AZT blocks reverse transcriptase; there is no cure or vaccine.


### 2. Bioenergetics

**bio-02-c01 — Glucose respiration: where and how much** _(map)_

| Stage | Site | Per glucose |
|---|---|---|
| Glycolysis | Cytoplasm | 2 pyruvate, net 2 ATP, 2 NADH (uses 2 ATP first) |
| Link reaction | Matrix | 2 acetyl CoA, 2 CO₂, 2 NADH |
| Krebs cycle (×2 turns) | Matrix | 4 CO₂, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂, 2 ATP |
| ETC + ATP synthase | Inner membrane (cristae) | Most ATP; O₂ → H₂O |

One Krebs turn: acetyl CoA (2C) + oxaloacetate (4C) → citrate (6C); releases 2 CO₂, 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 ATP.

💡 NADH ≈ 3 ATP, FADH₂ ≈ 2 ATP. O₂ is the final electron acceptor; cyanide blocks cytochrome oxidase and stops the chain.

⚠️ No O₂ → only glycolysis, net 2 ATP. Muscle: pyruvate → lactic acid. Yeast: → ethanol + CO₂. Both regenerate NAD⁺.


**bio-02-c02 — Proteins and fats as fuel: entry points** _(trick)_

Everything funnels into acetyl CoA or a Krebs/glycolysis intermediate.

| Fuel | First step | Enters as |
|---|---|---|
| Amino acids | Deamination (liver): NH₂ → NH₃ → urea | Alanine → pyruvate; glutamate → α-ketoglutarate; aspartate → oxaloacetate |
| Glycerol | Lipase splits fat | PGAL (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate) → glycolysis |
| Fatty acids | β-oxidation (mitochondrial matrix) | 2C acetyl CoA units → Krebs |

💡 β-oxidation count: n-carbon fatty acid → n/2 acetyl CoA in (n/2 − 1) rounds. Palmitic acid (16C) → 8 acetyl CoA, 7 rounds. Each round gives 1 NADH + 1 FADH₂.

⚠️ Fat gives about twice the energy of carbohydrate per gram (more H, less O). RQ: carbohydrate 1.0, protein ≈ 0.9, fat ≈ 0.7.


### 3. Biological Molecules

**bio-03-c01 — Water: property → why it matters** _(table)_

Water is 70–90% of a cell. Polar (O δ−, H δ+), bond angle 104.5°; each molecule can H-bond with up to 4 others.

| Property | Biological use |
|---|---|
| Solvent for polar/ionic substances | Transport in blood and xylem; reactions in solution |
| Reagent | Hydrolysis; photosynthesis (water is split, gives O₂) |
| High specific heat (1 cal/g/°C) | Body and ponds resist temperature change |
| High heat of vaporisation | Sweating and transpiration cool |
| Max density at 4 °C; ice floats | Ice insulates pond; fish survive below |
| Cohesion and adhesion | Water column in xylem; surface tension |
| Slight ionization | H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻; [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = 10⁻⁷ mol/L, neutral |

💡 Almost every answer here traces back to one cause: hydrogen bonding.


**bio-03-c02 — Sugars and polysaccharides at a glance** _(table)_

| Carbohydrate | Made of | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Maltose | glucose + glucose (α1→4) | Reducing |
| Sucrose | glucose + fructose | Non-reducing; transported in phloem |
| Lactose | glucose + galactose | Milk sugar; reducing |
| Starch | α-glucose: amylose (unbranched) + amylopectin (α1→6 branches) | Plant store; iodine → blue-black |
| Glycogen | α-glucose, more branched than amylopectin | Animal starch; liver and muscle |
| Cellulose | β-glucose, β1→4, unbranched | Plant wall; most abundant carbohydrate |
| Chitin | N-acetylglucosamine | Arthropod exoskeleton, fungal wall |

💡 Glucose is an aldose (aldohexose), fructose a ketose (ketohexose); same formula C₆H₁₂O₆, so isomers. Maltose = C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ (one H₂O lost).

⚠️ Humans lack cellulase, so cannot break the β1→4 glucose links of cellulose (lactase does split the β-link in lactose).


**bio-03-c03 — Four levels of protein structure** _(table)_

| Level | What it is | Held by | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Amino acid sequence | Peptide bonds | Insulin: 51 aa, chains A (21) + B (30); Sanger |
| Secondary | α-helix or β-pleated sheet | H-bonds (C=O…H–N) | Keratin (α); silk fibroin (β) |
| Tertiary | 3-D folding of one chain | Disulphide, ionic, H-bonds, hydrophobic | Myoglobin |
| Quaternary | Two or more chains together | Same bonds as tertiary | Haemoglobin: 2α + 2β |

💡 n amino acids in one chain → n − 1 peptide bonds and n − 1 H₂O released. Disulphide bridge (cysteine–cysteine) is the only covalent bond in tertiary structure.

⚠️ Denaturation (heat, pH, heavy metals) breaks 2°–4° structure but leaves the primary sequence intact. α-helix and β-sheet: Pauling and Corey.


**bio-03-c04 — DNA numbers and base-pairing sums** _(formula)_

- Watson and Crick, 1953 (X-ray data from Franklin and Wilkins)
- A=T: 2 H-bonds; G≡C: 3 H-bonds; purine always pairs with pyrimidine
- Chargaff: A = T, G = C, so A + G = T + C = 50%
- Rise per base pair 0.34 nm; 10 bp per turn; 3.4 nm per turn; diameter 2 nm
- Strands antiparallel (5′→3′ against 3′→5′), joined within a strand by phosphodiester bonds

Example: A = 20% → T = 20%, G = C = 30%. Length of 1000 bp = 1000 × 0.34 nm = 340 nm.

💡 H-bonds = 2 × (A–T pairs) + 3 × (G–C pairs). More G–C → harder to separate strands.

⚠️ RNA swaps thymine for uracil and deoxyribose for ribose. Chargaff's rule does not apply to single-stranded mRNA.


### 4. Cell Structure & Function

**bio-04-c01 — Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic cells** _(table)_

| Feature | Prokaryote (bacteria) | Eukaryote |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | None; DNA in nucleoid | Membrane-bound nucleus |
| DNA | Circular, no histones; plus plasmids | Linear, with histones |
| Ribosomes | 70S (50S + 30S) | 80S (60S + 40S) in cytoplasm |
| Wall | Peptidoglycan (murein) | Plants cellulose; fungi chitin; animals none |
| Organelles | No mitochondria, ER, Golgi | Present |
| Size | About 1–10 μm | About 10–100 μm |
| Division | Binary fission | Mitosis / meiosis |

💡 Respiration enzymes in bacteria sit on the cell membrane. Mitochondria and chloroplasts have 70S ribosomes and circular DNA, like bacteria.

⚠️ Gram-positive: thick peptidoglycan. Gram-negative: thin layer + outer lipopolysaccharide membrane. Mycoplasma has no wall at all.


**bio-04-c02 — Organelles: job and exam hook** _(table)_

| Organelle | Main job | Exam hook |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Holds DNA; controls cell | Double envelope with pores; outer membrane continuous with ER |
| Nucleolus | Makes rRNA, assembles ribosomes | Large in protein-making cells |
| Rough ER | Protein synthesis for export | Ribosomes attached; rich in pancreas |
| Smooth ER | Lipid and steroid synthesis, detox | Liver, adrenal cortex, testes; muscle = sarcoplasmic reticulum (Ca²⁺) |
| Golgi (Camillo Golgi, 1898) | Modify (add sugars), package, secrete | Makes lysosomes; cell plate in plants |
| Mitochondria | Aerobic respiration | Krebs in matrix; ETC on cristae; own DNA; maternal |

💡 Secretory route: RER → vesicle → Golgi (cis face in, trans face out) → secretory vesicle → plasma membrane.


**bio-04-c03 — Chromosome types by centromere** _(mnemonic)_

> **M-S-A-T: Middle, Slightly off, Almost at the end, Tip**
> - M = Metacentric: centromere in the Middle, arms equal
> - S = Submetacentric: Slightly off centre, one arm a bit longer
> - A = Acrocentric: Almost at the end, one arm very short
> - T = Telocentric: at the Tip, one arm only; not found in humans

Chromosome = DNA + histone proteins (chromatin). Nucleosome = DNA wound round 8 histones; H1 sits on linker DNA. Ends = telomeres.

💡 Humans: 46 in body cells (44 autosomes + XX or XY), 23 in gametes. Karyotypes are made at metaphase. Trisomy 21 (47) = Down syndrome.

⚠️ Euchromatin: loosely coiled, light-staining, active. Heterochromatin: tightly coiled, dark, mostly inactive (e.g. Barr body).


### 5. Coordination & Control

**bio-05-c01 — Receptor types** _(table)_

Receptors are transducers: they change stimulus energy into nerve impulses (via a receptor/generator potential).

| Type | Detects | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanoreceptor | touch, pressure, sound, stretch | Meissner's, Pacinian, cochlear hair cells, baroreceptors, muscle spindles |
| Chemoreceptor | chemicals | taste buds, olfactory cells, carotid & aortic bodies (CO₂, O₂, pH) |
| Thermoreceptor | heat, cold | free nerve endings in skin |
| Nociceptor | damaging stimuli → pain | free (naked) nerve endings |
| Photoreceptor | light | rods (dim light, no colour), cones (colour) |

- Meissner's corpuscle: light touch, just under the epidermis (many in fingertips, lips).
- Pacinian corpuscle: deep pressure, deep dermis; onion-like layers around a nerve ending.

💡 A stronger stimulus gives MORE impulses per second, not bigger impulses.


**bio-05-c02 — Nerve impulse step by step** _(map)_

- Resting (≈ −70 mV): inside negative. Na⁺ high outside, K⁺ high inside. Na⁺/K⁺ pump: 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in per ATP.
- Threshold stimulus → Na⁺ channels open → Na⁺ rushes IN → depolarisation (inside becomes +).
- K⁺ channels open → K⁺ moves OUT → repolarisation.
- K⁺ channels close slowly → membrane dips below rest (hyperpolarisation, 'undershoot') → Na⁺/K⁺ pump keeps the ion gradients → back to resting potential.

- All-or-none: below threshold = no impulse; above = full-size impulse.
- Myelinated axon: impulse jumps node to node (saltatory) → fastest in thick myelinated fibres.
- Synapse: Ca²⁺ enters knob → vesicles release neurotransmitter (e.g. acetylcholine) → one-way only, because vesicles are only presynaptic.

⚠️ Block acetylcholinesterase → ACh is not broken down → continuous stimulation of the next cell.


**bio-05-c03 — Reflex arc and reflex types** _(trick)_

Arc: receptor → sensory neuron (dorsal root; cell body in dorsal root ganglion) → interneuron (grey matter) → motor neuron (ventral root; cell body in ventral horn) → effector.

| Basis | Types | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Centre | spinal / cranial | knee jerk / pupil narrowing in bright light |
| Learning | unconditioned (inborn) / conditioned (learned) | baby's grasp / Pavlov's dog salivating at a bell |
| Synapses | monosynaptic / polysynaptic | knee jerk / withdrawal from heat |

💡 Knee jerk = 2 neurons, 1 synapse, no interneuron; effector = quadriceps (thigh) muscle, so the leg kicks.

⚠️ Cut dorsal roots → no sensation and no reflex. A spinal reflex below a cut spinal cord still works.


**bio-05-c04 — Brain parts and jobs** _(table)_

| Part | Division | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| Cerebrum | forebrain | thinking, memory, senses, voluntary movement |
| Thalamus | forebrain | relay station for sensory impulses to cortex |
| Hypothalamus | forebrain | thirst, hunger, body temperature; controls pituitary |
| Midbrain | midbrain | visual and auditory reflexes (turning head to a sound or flash) |
| Cerebellum | hindbrain | balance, smooth coordinated movement |
| Pons | hindbrain | relay; helps control breathing |

- Lobes: frontal (motor area in front of central sulcus, Broca's speech), parietal (touch/pain sensory area behind it), temporal (hearing, Wernicke's understanding), occipital (vision).
- Brainstem = midbrain + pons + medulla. Corpus callosum joins the hemispheres; limbic system (hippocampus) = emotion and memory.

⚠️ Control is crossed: left motor area damage → weakness on the RIGHT side.


### 6. Enzymes

**bio-06-c01 — Enzyme parts and models** _(table)_

Apoenzyme (protein only, inactive) + cofactor = holoenzyme (active).

| Cofactor | Nature | Binding | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prosthetic group | organic | tight, covalent, permanent | haem of cytochromes |
| Coenzyme | organic (often from vitamins) | loose, detachable | NAD⁺, FAD |
| Activator | inorganic metal ion | detachable | Mg²⁺, Zn²⁺, Fe²⁺ |

- Lock and key (Emil Fischer): rigid active site fits one substrate.
- Induced fit (Koshland): active site changes shape around the substrate; explains enzymes that act on related substrates.

💡 Enzymes speed reactions by lowering activation energy; they are not used up and do not change the products or ΔH.


**bio-06-c02 — Rate graphs: temperature, pH, concentration** _(trick)_

- Temperature: rate rises to the optimum (≈37 °C for human enzymes), then falls sharply as H-bonds break and the enzyme denatures.
- Cold only slows enzymes (reversible); boiling denatures them for good.
- pH: bell-shaped curve; wrong pH changes charges in the active site.
- Enzyme concentration (substrate in excess): rate rises in a straight line.
- Substrate concentration: rate rises, then levels off when all active sites are full (saturation).

| Enzyme | Optimum pH |
|---|---|
| Pepsin | 2.0 |
| Salivary amylase | 6.8 |
| Pancreatic lipase | 9.0 |

⚠️ At saturation, adding more substrate does nothing; only more enzyme raises the rate.


**bio-06-c03 — Enzyme inhibitors** _(table)_

| Type | Binds to | Beaten by more substrate? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competitive | active site (looks like substrate) | yes | malonate on succinate dehydrogenase |
| Non-competitive | another site; changes enzyme shape | no | heavy-metal ions |
| Irreversible | enzyme, by strong covalent bonds | no; enzyme lost for good | nerve gases on acetylcholinesterase |

Feedback inhibition: the end product binds the allosteric site of the FIRST enzyme in its pathway; when the product level falls, the enzyme works again. It stops waste of raw materials.

💡 Inhibition strong at low substrate but gone at high substrate = competitive.


### 7. Evolution

**bio-07-c01 — Origin of life: chemical evolution** _(map)_

Earth ≈ 4.6 billion years old. Early atmosphere: CH₄, NH₃, H₂, H₂O vapour, no free O₂.

- Gases → (lightning, UV light, heat) → simple organic monomers (amino acids, sugars, bases)
- Monomers → polymers (proteins, nucleic acids)
- Polymers cluster in water → coacervates (Oparin)
- Coacervates → first cells: anaerobic, heterotrophic prokaryotes
- Later: photosynthesis → free O₂ → ozone layer, aerobic life

💡 Miller–Urey (1953) passed sparks through CH₄ + NH₃ + H₂ + H₂O; after a week the water held amino acids.

⚠️ Organic molecules can't build up today: O₂ oxidises them and microbes eat them. RNA was likely the first hereditary molecule (it can store information and act as a catalyst).


**bio-07-c02 — Lamarck vs Darwin** _(table)_

|  | Lamarck | Darwin |
|---|---|---|
| Book | Philosophie Zoologique (1809) | On the Origin of Species (1859) |
| Key idea | use and disuse; inheritance of acquired characters | natural selection of inherited variations |
| Giraffe | stretched its neck; longer neck passed on | long-necked variants survived and bred more |
| Influences | - | Malthus (population outgrows food), Galápagos finches, pigeon breeders |
| Status | rejected | accepted; updated as Neo-Darwinism (genetics + mutation) |

Weismann cut the tails of mice for many generations; babies were still born with tails. Germplasm theory: only changes in germ (sex) cells are inherited.

💡 'Survival of the fittest' was coined by Herbert Spencer, not Darwin.


**bio-07-c03 — Natural selection in action** _(trick)_

- 1. Overproduction of offspring, yet population size stays fairly steady
- 2. Struggle for existence, fiercest within the same species
- 3. Variation, and only inherited variation matters
- 4. Survival of the fittest: fittest = leaves the most offspring
- 5. Favourable traits accumulate → new species

| Example | Selecting agent |
|---|---|
| Peppered moth: dark form spread with soot, light form returned after clean air | birds eating visible moths |
| Antibiotic-resistant bacteria | antibiotic |
| DDT-resistant houseflies | DDT |
| Sickle-cell allele common in malaria areas | malaria |

⚠️ The drug or spray does NOT cause resistance; it kills the rest, leaving resistant variants that were already there. No variation = no selection.


### 8. Reproduction

**bio-08-c01 — Male system: SEVEN UP** _(mnemonic)_

> **SEVEN UP: the path of sperm**
> - S: Seminiferous tubules (testis), where sperm are made
> - E: Epididymis, sperm mature and are stored
> - V: Vas deferens
> - E: Ejaculatory duct (vas deferens + seminal vesicle duct)
> - N: Nothing
> - U: Urethra
> - P: Penis

Glands: seminal vesicles, prostate (around urethra below bladder), Cowper's (bulbourethral; mucus).

Hypothalamus GnRH → pituitary FSH + LH. LH → Leydig (interstitial) cells → testosterone. FSH + testosterone → spermatogenesis. High testosterone → less GnRH and LH (negative feedback).

💡 1 primary spermatocyte → 4 sperm; 1 primary oocyte → 1 ovum + polar bodies.


**bio-08-c02 — Menstrual cycle (28 days)** _(table)_

| Days | Phase | Hormones | Uterus lining |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Menstrual | oestrogen and progesterone low | shed (bleeding) |
| 6–13 | Follicular (proliferative) | FSH → follicle grows → oestrogen rises | rebuilds |
| ~14 | Ovulation | LH surge (high oestrogen: positive feedback) | thick |
| 15–28 | Luteal (secretory) | corpus luteum → progesterone (+ oestrogen); FSH, LH low | thick, glandular, ready for implantation |

- No fertilisation → corpus luteum breaks down → progesterone falls → menstruation.
- Pregnancy → hCG keeps the corpus luteum alive → no menstruation.

⚠️ Ovulation releases a SECONDARY oocyte; fertilisation is in the oviduct. Bleeding starts 1 March → ovulation ≈ 14 March.


**bio-08-c03 — Sexually transmitted diseases** _(table)_

| Disease | Cause | Key signs |
|---|---|---|
| Gonorrhoea | Neisseria gonorrhoeae (Gram-negative diplococcus) | pus discharge, burning urination; women often have no symptoms |
| Syphilis | Treponema pallidum (spirochaete) | painless chancre → skin rash → years later heart, brain and nerve damage |
| Genital herpes | Herpes simplex virus (HSV-2) | painful blisters that come back; virus hides in nerve ganglia |
| AIDS | HIV (retrovirus) | destroys helper T cells → opportunistic infections and cancers (Kaposi's sarcoma) |

⚠️ The syphilis chancre heals by itself but the infection goes on. Treponema can cross the placenta to the fetus.

💡 Bacteria (gonorrhoea, syphilis) respond to antibiotics; viruses (herpes, HIV) do not.


### 9. Support & Movement

**bio-09-c01 — Three cartilages and three bone cells** _(table)_

| Cartilage | Matrix fibres | Perichondrium | Found in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyaline | Fine collagen (glassy look) | Yes (not on joint surfaces) | Trachea rings, nose, ends of long bones |
| Elastic | Many elastic fibres | Yes | Pinna, epiglottis |
| Fibrocartilage | Thick collagen bundles (toughest) | No | Intervertebral discs, pubic symphysis |

- Osteoblast = bone Builder (secretes matrix)
- Osteocyte = blast trapped in a lacuna (maintains bone)
- Osteoclast = Clears bone (large, multinucleate, resorbs matrix)

⚠️ Cartilage has no blood vessels: chondrocytes get food by diffusion through the matrix, so cartilage heals slowly.


**bio-09-c02 — Skeletal vs cardiac vs smooth muscle** _(table)_

| Feature | Skeletal | Cardiac | Smooth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell shape | Long cylinders, unbranched | Branched | Spindle, unbranched |
| Nuclei | Many, at edge | One (or two), central | One, central |
| Striations | Yes | Yes | No |
| Control | Voluntary | Involuntary (myogenic) | Involuntary (autonomic) |
| Special feature | Fibres = multinucleate cells | Intercalated discs | Walls of gut, vessels, uterus |
| Fatigue | Fatigues quickly | Does not fatigue (rests in each diastole) | Fatigues very little (slow, sustained contraction) |

💡 Striated + involuntary = cardiac only. Intercalated discs let the impulse pass quickly so heart cells contract together.


**bio-09-c03 — Sliding filament: from nerve to contraction** _(map)_

- Nerve impulse → acetylcholine released at neuromuscular junction
- → sarcolemma depolarised → impulse runs down T-tubules
- → sarcoplasmic reticulum releases Ca²⁺
- → Ca²⁺ binds troponin → tropomyosin moves off actin's binding sites
- → myosin heads bind actin (cross-bridges) → power stroke pulls thin filaments to the centre
- → new ATP binds myosin → head detaches → ATP split, head re-cocks

Thick filament = myosin. Thin filament = actin + tropomyosin + troponin. Sarcomere = Z line to Z line.

💡 On contraction, H zone and I band shorten ("HI shrink"); the A band stays the same length; Z lines move closer.

⚠️ Rigor mortis: no ATP after death, so myosin heads cannot detach from actin.

|---|---|---|
| Fibrous | Immovable | Skull sutures, teeth in sockets |
| Cartilaginous | Slight | Between vertebral bodies, pubic symphysis |
| Synovial: ball-and-socket | All directions | Hip, shoulder |
| Synovial: hinge | One plane | Elbow, knee |
| Synovial: saddle | Two planes | Base of thumb |

| Arthritis | Cause | Clue |
|---|---|---|

⚠️ Ligament = bone to bone; tendon = muscle to bone.


### 10. Inheritance

**bio-10-c01 — Mendel's ratios at a glance** _(table)_

| Cross | Phenotype ratio | Genotype ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Monohybrid F2 (Tt × Tt) | 3 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 |
| Monohybrid test cross (Tt × tt) | 1 : 1 | 1 : 1 |
| Dihybrid F2 (RrYy × RrYy) | 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 | 9 genotypes |
| Dihybrid test cross (RrYy × rryy) | 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 | 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 |
| Incomplete dominance F2 (red × white → pink) | 1 : 2 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 |

Segregation: the two alleles separate into different gametes (anaphase I). Independent assortment: genes on different chromosomes sort into gametes independently.

💡 Of the tall F2 plants, 2/3 are heterozygous. A test cross giving all dominant offspring means the parent was homozygous (TT).


**bio-10-c02 — Fast gamete and probability counts** _(trick)_

*Gamete types = 2ⁿ*

n = number of heterozygous gene pairs (homozygous pairs add nothing).

- TT → 2⁰ = 1 type
- RrYy → 2² = 4 types (RY, Ry, rY, ry)
- AaBBCcDd → 2³ = 8 types (BB is homozygous)

*Product rule*

Split a dihybrid into two monohybrids and multiply. RrYy × RrYy: round green = 3/4 × 1/4 = 3/16; RrYy offspring = 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4.

💡 Punnett square boxes = (gamete types of parent 1) × (gamete types of parent 2): RrYy × RrYy = 4 × 4 = 16.


**bio-10-c03 — Linkage, crossing over and map units** _(formula)_

Recombination frequency (%) = (recombinant offspring ÷ total offspring) × 100

- 1 map unit (centimorgan) = 1% recombination
- Crossing over: between non-sister chromatids in prophase I (pachytene); chiasmata visible in diplotene
- Linked genes: parental classes outnumber recombinant classes in a test cross
- No crossing over: coupling dihybrid AB/ab gives only 2 gamete types (AB, ab)
- Morgan used Drosophila (4 pairs of chromosomes)

💡 Gene order: the two genes with the largest distance are at the ends. A–B 12, B–C 5, A–C 17 → order A–B–C.

⚠️ Recombination never exceeds 50%; 50% looks like independent assortment (genes far apart or on different chromosomes).


**bio-10-c04 — X-linked recessive rules (hemophilia, colour blindness)** _(trick)_

- Father never passes an X-linked allele to his son (son gets his Y)
- Affected father × normal mother → all daughters carriers, all sons normal
- Carrier mother × normal father → ½ sons affected, ½ daughters carriers
- Affected son of normal parents got the allele from his mother
- More common in males: one X, so one recessive allele shows

Hemophilia A = factor VIII missing; hemophilia B (Christmas disease) = factor IX. Blood clots very slowly.

💡 Y-linked (holandric) traits pass from father to all sons and never to daughters.

⚠️ Read the question: "proportion of sons" (½) is not "proportion of all children" (¼).


### 11. Circulation

**bio-11-c01 — Heart: valves and the impulse pathway** _(map)_

- Right side: right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle → pulmonary semilunar valve → pulmonary trunk
- Left side: 4 pulmonary veins → left atrium → bicuspid (mitral) valve → left ventricle → aortic semilunar valve → aorta
- Chordae tendineae (anchored to papillary muscles) stop AV valves turning inside out

Impulse: SA node (pacemaker, wall of right atrium) → atria contract → AV node (short delay) → bundle of His → Purkinje fibres → ventricles contract.

💡 "Lubb" = AV valves closing (start of ventricular systole); "dub" = semilunar valves closing.

⚠️ Heart is myogenic: the beat starts in the SA node itself. Vagus nerve slows it; sympathetic nerves speed it up.


**bio-11-c02 — Cardiac cycle timings at 75 beats/min** _(table)_

| Phase | Time (s) |
|---|---|
| Whole cycle (60 ÷ 75) | 0.8 |
| Atrial systole | 0.1 |
| Ventricular systole | 0.3 |
| Complete diastole (all relaxed) | 0.4 |
| Ventricles relaxed in total | 0.5 |
| Atria relaxed in total | 0.7 |

ECG: P wave = atrial depolarisation; QRS = ventricular depolarisation; T wave = ventricular repolarisation.

💡 Most ventricular filling happens passively during complete diastole, before the atria contract.

⚠️ Left ventricle has the thickest wall: it pumps blood round the whole body. Normal adult BP is about 120/80 mmHg.


**bio-11-c03 — Arteries, veins, capillaries and lymph** _(table)_

| Feature | Artery | Vein | Capillary |
|---|---|---|---|

**bio-12-c01 — Five antibody classes** _(table)_

### 13. Respiration

**bio-13-c01 — How blood carries O₂ and CO₂** _(trick)_

*Oxygen*

- About 97% as oxyhaemoglobin, about 3% dissolved in plasma
- O₂ binds the iron (Fe²⁺) of haem; one Hb carries at most 4 O₂

*Carbon dioxide*

- About 70% as bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) in plasma
- About 23% as carbaminohaemoglobin (CO₂ on the globin part)
- About 7% dissolved in plasma
- Carbonic anhydrase in RBCs: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
- HCO₃⁻ leaves the RBC, Cl⁻ enters: the chloride shift

💡 Bohr effect: more CO₂ / more H⁺ (lower pH) shifts the O₂ curve to the RIGHT, so Hb releases O₂ to busy tissues. Higher temperature also shifts it right.


**bio-13-c02 — Lung volumes** _(formula)_

| Volume | Meaning | Typical adult |
|---|---|---|
| Tidal volume (TV) | in or out in one quiet breath | ≈ 500 mL |
| Inspiratory reserve (IRV) | extra air you can force in | ≈ 3000 mL |
| Expiratory reserve (ERV) | extra air you can force out | ≈ 1100 mL |
| Residual volume (RV) | left after the hardest breath out | ≈ 1200 mL |

- Vital capacity VC = TV + IRV + ERV
- Total lung capacity TLC = VC + RV
- Air breathed per minute = breathing rate × TV (16 × 500 = 8000 mL)

⚠️ Residual volume is NOT part of vital capacity. Books differ slightly in the typical values; the sums are what MCQs test.


**bio-13-c03 — Smoking: what does what** _(table)_

| In smoke / disease | Main effect |
|---|---|
| Nicotine | addictive; raises heart rate and BP, narrows arteries, makes platelets sticky (clots) |
| Carbon monoxide | binds Hb about 200–250× more tightly than O₂ → carboxyhaemoglobin, less O₂ carried |
| Tar | holds most carcinogens; paralyses and destroys cilia |
| Chronic bronchitis | extra mucus + no cilia → blocked, inflamed bronchi, smoker's cough |
| Emphysema | phagocyte enzymes break alveolar walls → less surface area, lost elastic recoil |
| Lung cancer | carcinogens in tar; usually starts in bronchial epithelium |

💡 COPD = chronic bronchitis + emphysema. Emphysema damage is permanent and makes breathing OUT hard.

**bio-14-c02 — Gut hormones: who calls whom** _(trick)_

- Secretin: made in the duodenum when acid chyme arrives → pancreas releases watery bicarbonate-rich juice
- Cholecystokinin (CCK): made in the duodenum after a fatty meal → gall bladder contracts, pancreas releases enzymes

Secretin was the first hormone ever discovered (Bayliss and Starling, 1902).


### 15. Homeostasis

**bio-15-c01 — Down the nephron** _(map)_

- Glomerulus → Bowman's capsule: ultrafiltration (no blood cells, no plasma proteins)
- → PCT: reabsorbs ALL glucose and amino acids, most water and salts; brush border + many mitochondria
- → Descending limb: permeable to water, water leaves
- → Ascending limb: pumps out NaCl, impermeable to water (filtrate leaves hypotonic)
- → DCT: aldosterone → more Na⁺ in, K⁺ out; H⁺ secreted
- → Collecting duct: ADH → more water reabsorbed
- → Renal pelvis → ureter → bladder → urethra

💡 Three steps in order: filtration → selective reabsorption → tubular secretion. About 180 L filtrate a day becomes about 1.5 L urine.


**bio-15-c02 — Glomerular vs peritubular capillaries** _(table)_

| Feature | Glomerular | Peritubular |
|---|---|---|
| Blood comes from | afferent arteriole | efferent arteriole |
| Blood pressure | high (efferent is narrower) | low |
| Walls | fenestrated (many pores) | ordinary |
| Main job | filtration | take back reabsorbed fluid; supply secreted substances |
| Drains into | efferent arteriole | renal vein branches |

💡 Net filtration pressure = blood pressure − (plasma protein osmotic pressure + capsule pressure). Example: 55 − (30 + 15) = 10 mmHg.

Vasa recta = peritubular loops beside the long loops of Henle of juxtamedullary nephrons.


**bio-15-c03 — Ammonia vs urea vs uric acid** _(table)_

|  | Ammonia | Urea | Uric acid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toxicity | highest | medium | lowest |
| Water needed | most | medium | least (semi-solid) |
| Energy to make | least | medium | most |
| Animals | bony fish, aquatic invertebrates, tadpoles | mammals, adult frogs, sharks | birds, reptiles, insects |

💡 Urea is made in the LIVER by the ornithine (urea) cycle: ornithine → citrulline → arginine → urea + ornithine (enzyme arginase). Kidneys only remove it.

⚠️ In humans uric acid comes from purine (nucleic acid) breakdown; too much → gout. Creatinine comes from creatine in muscle.


**bio-15-c04 — Kidney stones and dialysis** _(trick)_

*Stones (renal calculi)*

- Commonest: calcium oxalate. Uric acid stones: gout, acidic urine. Struvite (Mg NH₄ phosphate): infection, alkaline urine
- Main risk: too little water; also oxalate-rich food, high calcium in the urine
- Lithotripsy = shock waves break the stone; bits pass out in urine. Big stones → keyhole surgery

*Haemodialysis*

- Dialysis fluid: glucose and salts at normal plasma levels, NO urea
- Partially permeable membrane; countercurrent flow keeps a gradient
- Heparin stops the blood clotting in the machine

💡 Peritoneal dialysis uses the patient's own peritoneum as the membrane.


**bio-15-c05 — Thermoregulation: hot vs cold** _(table)_

Thermostat: the hypothalamus, set near 37 °C. It works by negative feedback, so enzymes stay near their best temperature.

| Response | Too hot | Too cold |
|---|---|---|
| Skin arterioles | vasodilation: more blood to skin, flushed | vasoconstriction: less heat lost |
| Sweating | more: evaporation removes heat | less |
| Muscles | relaxed | shivering makes heat |
| Hair | lies flat | erect: traps a layer of air |
| Metabolism | normal | raised (thyroxine; brown fat in babies) |

💡 Endotherms (birds, mammals) make their own heat; ectotherms (fish, amphibians, reptiles) depend on outside heat, e.g. a lizard basking in the sun.

⚠️ Fever: pyrogens raise the set point, so you shiver and feel cold while temperature rises. Humid air slows sweat evaporation, so cooling is poorer.

**bio-16-c02 — Diagnosis: probes, PCR, monoclonals** _(trick)_

- Pregnancy kits: monoclonals detect hCG, made by the placenta/embryo

⚠️ Sample DNA must be denatured (made single-stranded) before a probe can bind. A probe 5'-AGCTTG-3' binds 3'-TCGAAC-5'.

## Chemistry (51 cards)

### 1. Fundamental Concepts

**chem-01-c01 — The mole triangle: mass, particles, gas volume** _(formula)_

Moles (n) are the bridge. Convert everything to moles first, use the equation's mole ratio, then convert back.

| You have | Moles = | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Mass m | m / M | g ÷ g mol⁻¹ |
| Particles N | N / Nₐ | Nₐ = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ |
| Gas volume at STP | V / 22.4 | dm³ ÷ 22.4 dm³ mol⁻¹ |

- Mole ratio = ratio of coefficients in the BALANCED equation (N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃: H₂ : NH₃ = 3 : 2).
- Mass of one molecule = M / Nₐ (one H₂O ≈ 18 / 6.02 × 10²³ ≈ 3 × 10⁻²³ g).

⚠️ Coefficients give mole (and gas volume) ratios, never mass ratios. Balance first, then read the ratio.


**chem-01-c02 — Limiting reactant and percentage yield** _(trick)_

*Finding the limiting reactant*

- Divide the moles of each reactant by its coefficient.
- The smallest answer is the limiting reactant: it runs out first and fixes the maximum product.
- Excess left = amount taken − amount used by the limiting reactant.

*Yield*

% yield = (actual yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100. Theoretical yield is calculated from the limiting reactant; actual yield is what you really collect.

- Actual = % yield × theoretical ÷ 100
- Theoretical = actual × 100 ÷ % yield

💡 Actual < theoretical because of side reactions, incomplete (reversible) reactions and losses while handling. A yield above 100% means the product is impure or still wet.


### 2. Atomic Structure

**chem-02-c01 — Positive rays vs cathode rays** _(table)_

| Feature | Positive (canal) rays | Cathode rays |
|---|---|---|
| Discovered | Goldstein, 1886 (perforated cathode) | Studied by J. J. Thomson |
| Particles | Positive ions of the gas | Electrons |
| Travel | Towards the cathode, through its holes | From the cathode |
| Deflection | Towards the negative plate | Towards the positive plate |
| e/m | Depends on the gas; largest for H₂ (H⁺) | Same for every gas |

Proton: charge +1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, mass 1.6726 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, about 1836 times the electron's mass.

⚠️ Positive rays are made by electrons knocking electrons off gas atoms, so they come from the gas, not from the anode metal.


**chem-02-c02 — Photons and the Bohr hydrogen atom** _(formula)_

- Photon energy: E = hν = hc/λ = hc·ν̄ (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s)
- Wave number ν̄ = 1/λ, so E ∝ ν and E ∝ 1/λ: double λ, halve E
- Bohr's allowed orbits: mvr = nh/2π
- Radius rₙ = 0.529 n² Å (n = 2 → 2.12 Å)
- Energy Eₙ ∝ −1/n²; gaps shrink as n rises
- Emitted line: ΔE = E₂ − E₁ = hν (electron falls to a lower orbit)

| Series | Falls to n = | Region |
|---|---|---|
| Lyman | 1 | Ultraviolet |
| Balmer | 2 | Visible |
| Paschen | 3 | Infrared |
| Brackett | 4 | Infrared |
| Pfund | 5 | Infrared |

💡 The series is named by the LOWER level the electron lands on. Bohr's model works only for hydrogen and one-electron ions (He⁺, Li²⁺).


**chem-02-c03 — Quantum numbers and filling rules** _(trick)_

| Number | Values | Tells you |
|---|---|---|
| n | 1, 2, 3 … | Shell: n² orbitals, 2n² electrons |
| l | 0 to n − 1 (s, p, d, f) | Shape; subshell holds 2, 6, 10, 14 |
| m | −l … 0 … +l | Orientation (p has 3, d has 5) |
| s | +½ or −½ | Spin |

- Aufbau: lowest (n + l) fills first; if equal, lower n first (4s before 3d).
- Pauli: one orbital holds at most 2 electrons, opposite spins.
- Hund: degenerate orbitals fill singly with parallel spins before pairing (O has 2 unpaired).

⚠️ Exceptions: Cr = [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹, Cu = [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ (half-filled and full d are extra stable). Ions lose 4s before 3d: Fe²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁶, Fe³⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵.


### 3. Gases

**chem-03-c01 — Gas laws formula sheet** _(formula)_

| Law | Held constant | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Boyle | T, n | P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ (V ∝ 1/P) |
| Charles | P, n | V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ (V ∝ T) |
| Avogadro | P, T | V ∝ n |
| General | n | P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ |
| Ideal gas | — | PV = nRT |
| Density form | — | d = PM/RT; M = mRT/PV |

STP: 273 K (0 °C) and 1 atm = 760 mm Hg = 760 torr = 101325 Pa. One mole occupies 22.4 dm³.

Absolute zero: 0 K = −273.15 °C (often rounded to −273 °C). Charles: V changes by 1/273 of its 0 °C volume per °C.

⚠️ Always use kelvin in Charles and ideal gas sums. Doubling 100 °C does not double V; doubling 100 K does.


**chem-03-c02 — Values of the gas constant R** _(table)_

| Value of R | Units |
|---|---|
| 0.0821 | dm³ atm K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |
| 82.1 | cm³ atm K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |
| 62.4 | dm³ mm Hg (torr) K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |
| 8.314 | J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ (= N m K⁻¹ mol⁻¹) |
| 1.987 ≈ 2 | cal K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |

Origin: R = PV/nT = (1 atm × 22.4 dm³) ÷ (1 mol × 273 K) = 0.0821. Physically, R is work done per mole per kelvin.

💡 R is the same for every gas; only its number changes with the units. Converting: × 760 gives mm Hg, × 1000 gives cm³, ÷ 4.184 turns joules into calories.


**chem-03-c03 — Kinetic theory and why real gases deviate** _(trick)_

*Key postulates (ideal gas)*

- Molecules move randomly in straight lines between collisions.
- Collisions are perfectly elastic; wall collisions cause pressure.
- Average KE ∝ absolute temperature (same for all gases at one T).
- Molecules' own volume is negligible; no attractions between them.

rms speed = √(3RT/M): lighter gas moves faster (H₂ is 4 × faster than O₂); 4 × T gives 2 × speed.

| Condition | Faulty postulate | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| High pressure | Negligible volume | Real V larger than ideal |
| Low temperature | No attractions | Real P smaller than ideal |

💡 Real gases behave most ideally at LOW pressure and HIGH temperature. Easily liquefied gases (NH₃, SO₂, CO₂) deviate most; H₂ and He least.


### 4. Liquids

**chem-04-c01 — Evaporation vs boiling, and vapour pressure** _(table)_

| Feature | Evaporation | Boiling |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Any temperature | One fixed temperature |
| Where | Surface only | Throughout the liquid |
| Cause | Fastest surface molecules escape | Vapour pressure = external pressure |
| Effect | Cools the liquid left behind | T stays constant while heat is supplied |

Vapour pressure depends only on the nature of the liquid and its temperature, not on surface area or amount. Weaker intermolecular forces → higher vapour pressure → lower boiling point (ether > alcohol > water).

- Murree (low pressure): water boils below 100 °C.
- Pressure cooker (high pressure): water boils above 100 °C, food cooks faster.
- Vacuum distillation: lower pressure lets heat-sensitive liquids boil below their decomposition point.

⚠️ Surface area and wind change the RATE of evaporation, not the vapour pressure.


**chem-04-c02 — Hydrogen bonding and water's odd behaviour** _(trick)_

Hydrogen bond: attraction between H bonded to F, O or N and a lone pair on another F, O or N. Weaker than a covalent bond, stronger than dipole forces (London < dipole–dipole < H-bond).

| Compound | Boiling point | Why |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O | 100 °C | 2 H + 2 lone pairs → up to 4 H-bonds each |
| HF | ≈ 20 °C | Strongest single H-bond, but fewer per molecule |
| NH₃ | ≈ −33 °C | N less electronegative, 1 lone pair |
| H₂S | Gas | S not electronegative enough |

- Water is densest at 4 °C; it contracts from 0 to 4 °C, then expands.
- Ice ≈ 0.92 g cm⁻³: open hydrogen-bonded cage, so ice floats and lakes freeze from the top (fish survive).
- Water expands about 9% on freezing, so pipes burst.

💡 NH₃ dissolves well in water because it forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules.


### 5. Solids

**chem-05-c01 — Crystal properties, ionic vs molecular crystals** _(table)_

Crystalline solids: regular repeating order, definite shape, sharp melting point, cleavage planes, anisotropic (properties differ with direction). Amorphous solids (glass, plastic) soften over a range and are isotropic.

| Feature | Ionic crystal | Molecular crystal |
|---|---|---|
| Particles | Cations and anions | Molecules |
| Forces | Strong electrostatic | Weak (London, dipole, H-bond) |
| Melting point | High (NaCl 801 °C) | Low (I₂ 114 °C) |
| Conducts | Only molten or dissolved | No |
| Hardness | Hard but brittle | Soft, may sublime |
| Examples | NaCl, MgO, CsCl | I₂, ice, dry ice, sugar |

- Isomorphism: different substances, same crystal form (NaNO₃, CaCO₃).
- Polymorphism: one substance, different crystal forms (calcite and aragonite).
- Transition temperature: two forms coexist (rhombic ⇌ monoclinic sulphur, 95.5 °C).


**chem-05-c02 — Radius ratio, unit cell and lattice energy** _(trick)_

Two key factors in ionic crystal shape: the radius ratio r⁺/r⁻ and the charges on the ions.

| r⁺/r⁻ | Coordination no. | Geometry | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.225–0.414 | 4 | Tetrahedral | ZnS |
| 0.414–0.732 | 6 | Octahedral | NaCl |
| above 0.732 | 8 | Cubic | CsCl |

Unit cell: smallest repeating unit of the lattice; 7 crystal systems. Atoms per cell: simple cubic 1, face-centred cubic 4.

Lattice energy: energy released when 1 mole of ionic crystal forms from its gaseous ions, e.g. Na⁺(g) + Cl⁻(g) → NaCl(s). Found indirectly by the Born–Haber cycle.

💡 Higher charges and smaller ions → larger lattice energy → higher melting point: MgO > NaCl > KCl; NaF > NaCl > NaBr > NaI.


### 6. Chemical Equilibrium

**chem-06-c01 — Le Chatelier: which way it shifts** _(table)_

| Change | Equilibrium shifts | Kc changes? |
|---|---|---|
| Add reactant / remove product | Forward | No |
| Raise pressure (gases) | To side with fewer gas moles | No |
| Pressure change, equal gas moles (H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI) | No shift | No |
| Raise T, forward exothermic | Backward | Yes, Kc falls |
| Raise T, forward endothermic | Forward | Yes, Kc rises |
| Add catalyst | No shift; equilibrium reached sooner | No |

💡 Haber process: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, ΔH = −92 kJ. High pressure (about 200 atm) favours NH₃; a moderate 400–450 °C is a compromise between yield and rate; iron catalyst.

⚠️ Only temperature changes the value of Kc.


**chem-06-c02 — Kc, Qc and Ksp in one sheet** _(formula)_

- Kc = [products]^coefficients ÷ [reactants]^coefficients; leave out pure solids and liquids.
- Reverse the equation → new K = 1/Kc (Kc 50 → 0.02).
- Units: (mol dm⁻³)^Δn, Δn = total coefficients of products − reactants in the Kc expression. Δn = 0 → no units; N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ → mol⁻² dm⁶.
- Qc < Kc → goes forward; Qc > Kc → goes backward; Qc = Kc → at equilibrium.
- Ksp from solubility s: AB → s²; AB₂ or A₂B (CaF₂, Ag₂CrO₄) → 4s³; A₃B₂ (Ca₃(PO₄)₂) → 108s⁵.
- Precipitate forms when ionic product > Ksp.

💡 Very large Kc (10³⁰) → mixture is almost all products. Same formula type: smaller Ksp = less soluble.


**chem-06-c03 — Common ion effect and buffers** _(table)_

Common ion effect: adding an ion already present suppresses a weak electrolyte's ionisation or lowers a salt's solubility.

- HCl gas into saturated NaCl → pure NaCl crystallises.
- HCl with H₂S → low [S²⁻], so only group II sulphides precipitate.
- NH₄Cl with NH₄OH → low [OH⁻], so only group III hydroxides precipitate.

| Buffer | Made of | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Acidic (pH < 7) | Weak acid + its salt with strong base | CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa |
| Basic (pH > 7) | Weak base + its salt with strong acid | NH₄OH + NH₄Cl |
| Blood (pH ≈ 7.4) | Carbonic acid + bicarbonate | H₂CO₃ / HCO₃⁻ |

💡 pH = pKa + log([salt]/[acid]). Equal concentrations → pH = pKa (4.74 for acetic acid).

⚠️ Strong acid + its salt (HCl + NaCl) is NOT a buffer.


### 7. Reaction Kinetics

**chem-07-c01 — Order and units of k** _(table)_

Rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ. Overall order = m + n, found only by experiment, not from the balanced equation.

| Order | Double [A], rate becomes | Units of k |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Unchanged | mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ |
| 1 | ×2 | s⁻¹ |
| 2 | ×4 | mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹ |
| 3 | ×8 | mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹ |

💡 General unit of k: (mol dm⁻³)¹⁻ⁿ s⁻¹. Rate of a species ÷ its coefficient gives the rate of reaction. For N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃: NH₃ formed at 0.40 → rate of reaction 0.20 → H₂ used at 0.60 mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹.

⚠️ Order can be zero or a fraction; molecularity is always a whole number.


**chem-07-c02 — Half-life and the slow step** _(formula)_

- First order: t½ = 0.693/k, independent of starting concentration.
- General: t½ ∝ 1/[A]₀ⁿ⁻¹ (zero order: t½ grows with [A]₀).
- After n half-lives, fraction left = (½)ⁿ: 3 half-lives → 1/8.
- 0.80 → 0.10 in 60 min is three halvings, so t½ = 60 ÷ 3 = 20 min.
- Rate-determining step = the slowest step; the rate law comes from it.
- Pseudo-first order: one reactant in large excess (ester hydrolysis in water, sucrose inversion).

💡 A species made in one step and used up in the next (like a free X atom) is an intermediate, not a catalyst.


**chem-07-c03 — Activation energy and catalysts** _(trick)_

- Ea (forward) = peak energy − reactant energy.
- Ea (reverse) = Ea (forward) − ΔH.
- Exothermic: forward Ea < reverse Ea. Endothermic: forward Ea ≥ ΔH.
- Activated complex sits at the peak: bonds partly broken, partly formed.
- Catalyst lowers forward and reverse Ea by the same amount; ΔH, Kc and yield stay the same.
- A 10 K rise roughly doubles the rate: more molecules have energy ≥ Ea (the Maxwell–Boltzmann curve flattens and shifts right).

💡 Example: reactants 50, peak 150, products 20 kJ mol⁻¹ → Ea = 100, ΔH = −30, reverse Ea = 130.

⚠️ Light (H₂ + Cl₂ explodes in sunlight) and surface area are rate factors too; temperature works mainly by raising the fraction of molecules with energy ≥ Ea (Ea itself does not change), not by the small rise in collision count.


### 8. Thermochemistry & Energetics

**chem-08-c01 — Thermodynamic terms at a glance** _(table)_

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Open system | Exchanges matter and energy | Open beaker |
| Closed system | Exchanges energy only | Sealed flask |
| Isolated system | Exchanges neither | Sealed thermos flask |
| State function | Change depends only on start and end | H, E, P, V, T |
| Not a state function | Depends on the path | Heat q, work w |
| Exothermic | ΔH negative, heat given out | Combustion, neutralisation |
| Endothermic | ΔH positive, heat taken in | NH₄NO₃ dissolving, N₂ + O₂ → 2NO |

💡 Reverse a reaction → same size ΔH, opposite sign. Photosynthesis is endothermic; respiration is exothermic.


**chem-08-c02 — First law, heat and units** _(formula)_

- ΔE = q + w (FSc convention: q = heat absorbed, w = work done ON the system).
- Heat absorbed or work done on the system → +; heat lost or work done by the system → −.
- Constant volume: ΔE = qv (bomb calorimeter). Constant pressure: ΔH = qp.
- ΔH = ΔE + PΔV; ΔH ≈ ΔE when gas moles do not change (H₂ + I₂ → 2HI).
- Work of expansion = PΔV: 100 kPa × 0.010 m³ = 1000 J.
- Heat = m c ΔT: 50 g × 4.2 × 10 K = 2100 J.
- 1 J = 1 N m = 1 kg m² s⁻²; 1 cal = 4.184 J.

💡 Absorbs 200 J, does 50 J of work → ΔE = 200 + (−50) = +150 J. Isolated system → ΔE = 0.


**chem-08-c03 — Hess's law: build the cycle** _(trick)_

Total ΔH depends only on start and end, not on the route, because enthalpy is a state function.

- Reverse an equation → change the sign of ΔH.
- Multiply or halve an equation → do the same to ΔH.
- Add the steps → add their ΔH values.
- ΔH(reaction) = ΣΔHf(products) − ΣΔHf(reactants).
- ΔHf of an element in its standard state = 0.

💡 C + ½O₂ → CO: (C → CO₂, −394) minus (CO → CO₂, −283) = −111 kJ.

⚠️ The Born–Haber cycle (lattice energy) is just Hess's law applied to an ionic solid.


### 9. Electrochemistry

**chem-09-c01 — Redox and oxidation numbers** _(mnemonic)_

> **OIL RIG**
> - Oxidation Is Loss of electrons (oxidation number goes up)
> - Reduction Is Gain of electrons (oxidation number goes down)

- Free element (O₂, P₄, S₈, Na) = 0; F is −1 in all its compounds.
- O = −2, but −1 in peroxides (H₂O₂) and +2 in OF₂.
- H = +1, but −1 in metal hydrides (NaH).
- Sum = charge: S in H₂SO₄ +6, Mn in KMnO₄ +7, Cr in K₂Cr₂O₇ +6, N in NH₄⁺ −3, S in S₂O₃²⁻ +2.

💡 The oxidising agent is itself reduced; the reducing agent is itself oxidised. Electrons lost = electrons gained.

⚠️ Middle oxidation states (H₂O₂, SO₂) can act both ways; the lowest (H₂S, S = −2) can only reduce.


**chem-09-c02 — Electrodes, SHE and E°** _(table)_

|  | Galvanic cell | Electrolytic cell |
|---|---|---|
| Anode | Oxidation, negative | Oxidation, positive |
| Cathode | Reduction, positive | Reduction, negative |
| Energy | Chemical → electrical | Electrical → chemical |

- SHE: platinised Pt, H₂ at 1 atm, 1 M H⁺, 25 °C; E° = 0.00 V by convention.
- More negative E° = stronger reducing agent (Li); more positive = stronger oxidising agent (F₂).
- E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode: Daniell = 0.34 − (−0.76) = 1.10 V; electrons flow Zn → Cu.
- Metals with negative E° release H₂ from dilute acid; a lower-E° metal displaces a higher one.

💡 AN OX, RED CAT: oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode, in every cell.


### 10. Chemical Bonding

**chem-10-c01 — VSEPR shapes and angles** _(table)_

| Bond + lone pairs | Shape | Angle | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 + 0 | Linear | 180° | BeCl₂, CO₂ |
| 3 + 0 | Trigonal planar | 120° | BF₃, BCl₃ |
| 4 + 0 | Tetrahedral | 109.5° | CH₄, NH₄⁺ |
| 3 + 1 | Trigonal pyramidal | 107° | NH₃, H₃O⁺ |
| 2 + 2 | Bent (V-shaped) | 104.5° | H₂O |
| 5 + 0 | Trigonal bipyramidal | 120° and 90° | PCl₅ |
| 6 + 0 | Octahedral | 90° | SF₆ |

💡 Repulsion: lone–lone > lone–bond > bond–bond, so each lone pair squeezes the angle (109.5 → 107 → 104.5).

⚠️ SO₂ (2 bonding regions + 1 lone pair) is bent, not linear like CO₂.


**chem-10-c02 — Counting σ and π; hybridisation** _(trick)_

- Single bond = 1σ; double = 1σ + 1π; triple = 1σ + 2π.
- Ethene 5σ 1π; ethyne 3σ 2π; benzene 12σ 3π; CO₂ 2σ 2π.
- σ = head-on overlap (s–s, s–p, p–p or hybrid); π = sideways overlap of unhybridised p orbitals.
- π is weaker than σ and stops free rotation about C=C.

| Hybrid | s character | Angle | Carbon in |
|---|---|---|---|
| sp | 50% | 180° | C≡C (ethyne) |
| sp² | 33% | 120° | C=C (ethene) |
| sp³ | 25% | 109.5° | C–C (methane, ethane) |

💡 More s character → shorter, stronger bond: C–H in ethyne is shortest. Propene CH₃–CH=CH₂ is sp³, sp², sp².


**chem-10-c03 — Polarity, ionic character, bond energy** _(trick)_

Symmetrical shape → bond dipoles cancel → zero dipole moment, even with polar bonds.

- μ = 0: CO₂, BeCl₂, BF₃, CH₄, CCl₄, PCl₅, SF₆.
- Polar: H₂O, NH₃, SO₂, CHCl₃, HCl (bent or pyramidal, or unequal atoms).
- μ = charge × distance, unit debye (D). % ionic = observed μ ÷ 100%-ionic μ × 100 (1.2/6.0 → 20%).
- Ionic character grows with electronegativity difference; about 1.7 ≈ 50% ionic. H–H, Cl–Cl have none.
- Higher bond energy = stronger bond: C≡C > C=C > C–C; H–F > H–Cl > H–Br > H–I.

⚠️ A polar XY₂ molecule must be bent; a linear one would have zero dipole moment.


### 11. s- and p-Block Elements

**chem-11-c01 — Periodic trends and their exceptions** _(trick)_

| Property | Across a period → | Down a group ↓ |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius | decreases (more nuclear charge, same shell) | increases (new shell) |
| Ionisation energy | increases | decreases |
| Electronegativity | increases (F highest, 4.0) | decreases |

- IE dips: Mg > Al (3p¹ easy to remove) and N > O (paired 2p electron repels).
- Highest electron affinity: Cl, not F (F is tiny and crowded).
- Isoelectronic ions: more protons = smaller. O²⁻ > F⁻ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺.
- Covalent radius = half the bond length (Cl–Cl 198 pm → 99 pm).
- Blocks: last electron in ns = s, np = p, (n−1)d = d, (n−2)f = f.

⚠️ A big jump in successive IE marks a new inner shell: Na's 2nd IE is huge.


**chem-11-c02 — Group I and II metals: water and oxygen** _(table)_

| Metal | With water | Burnt in excess O₂ |
|---|---|---|
| Li | steady: LiOH + H₂ | Li₂O only (also Li₃N with N₂) |
| Na | vigorous: NaOH + H₂ | Na₂O₂ (peroxide) |
| K | violent, H₂ ignites | KO₂ (superoxide, O = −½) |
| Be | no reaction | BeO (amphoteric) |
| Mg | steam: MgO + H₂ | MgO; in air also Mg₃N₂ |
| Ca | cold water: Ca(OH)₂ + H₂ | CaO |
| Ba | fast: Ba(OH)₂ + H₂ | BaO₂ (peroxide) |

Reactivity rises down both groups. With Cl₂: MCl and MCl₂; LiCl and BeCl₂ are the most covalent (BeCl₂ forms polymeric chains).

💡 Diagonal pair Li–Mg: both form a nitride and a normal oxide only.


**chem-11-c03 — Group IV: oxidation states, chlorides, oxides** _(table)_

| Element | Type | More stable state | Oxide nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | non-metal | +4 | CO₂ acidic (gas, small molecules); CO neutral |
| Si | metalloid | +4 | SiO₂ acidic (giant covalent solid) |
| Ge | metalloid | +4 | GeO₂ amphoteric |
| Sn | metal | +4 (Sn²⁺ is a reducing agent) | SnO, SnO₂ amphoteric |
| Pb | metal | +2 (Pb⁴⁺ is an oxidising agent) | PbO, PbO₂ amphoteric |

Inert pair effect: down the group the ns² pair is held more tightly and stays out of bonding, so +2 becomes more stable than +4.

- CCl₄ is not hydrolysed: carbon has no vacant d orbitals for water to attack.
- SiCl₄ + 4H₂O → Si(OH)₄ + 4HCl: Si uses vacant 3d orbitals.
- PbCl₄ is the least stable tetrachloride: on gentle warming, PbCl₄ → PbCl₂ + Cl₂.

💡 Oxides change from acidic to amphoteric down the group as metallic character rises. Catenation is greatest for carbon.


### 12. Transition Elements

**chem-12-c01 — d-block configurations: the four traps** _(trick)_

- Exceptions: Cr = [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ and Cu = [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ (half-full and full 3d are extra stable).
- Making ions: 4s electrons leave FIRST, then 3d. Fe [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² → Fe²⁺ 3d⁶ → Fe³⁺ 3d⁵.
- Highest oxidation state = 4s + 3d electrons, up to Mn (+7). Cr in K₂Cr₂O₇ is +6.
- Sc shows only +3 (Sc³⁺ = [Ar]). Zn is not a typical transition element: Zn and Zn²⁺ both have full 3d¹⁰. Cu is one, because Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹.

First series: Sc (21) to Zn (30). General outer configuration (n−1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻².

⚠️ Variable oxidation states come from 4s and 3d being close in energy.


**chem-12-c02 — Unpaired electrons and magnetic moment** _(table)_

| Ion(s) | 3d | Unpaired n | μ (BM) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sc³⁺ | 3d⁰ | 0 | 0 |
| V²⁺, Cr³⁺ | 3d³ | 3 | 3.87 |
| Mn²⁺, Fe³⁺ | 3d⁵ | 5 | 5.92 |
| Fe²⁺, Co³⁺ | 3d⁶ | 4 | 4.90 |
| Ni²⁺ | 3d⁸ | 2 | 2.83 |
| Cu²⁺ | 3d⁹ | 1 | 1.73 |
| Zn²⁺, Cu⁺ | 3d¹⁰ | 0 | 0 |

Spin-only formula: μ = √(n(n + 2)) BM. Fill the five 3d boxes singly before pairing (Hund's rule).

💡 Most unpaired = 3d⁵ (5). Up to 3d⁵, n = number of d electrons; after it, n = 10 − d electrons.

| Aldehyde | –CHO | CH₃CHO ethanal |

**chem-13-c02 — Geometric vs optical isomerism tests** _(trick)_

*Geometric (cis–trans)*

- Needs a C=C (no free rotation) AND two different groups on EACH double-bond carbon.
- But-2-ene and ClCH=CHCl: yes. But-1-ene and propene (CH₂= end): no.
- cis-1,2-dichloroethene is polar (higher b.p.); trans is non-polar (higher m.p.).

*Optical*

- Needs a chiral carbon: four DIFFERENT groups, e.g. lactic acid CH₃CH(OH)COOH.
- Enantiomers: non-superimposable mirror images; same physical properties except they rotate plane-polarised light equally in opposite directions.
- Racemic mixture: 50 : 50 of both, rotations cancel → optically inactive.

💡 Count chiral C one by one: CH₃CH(OH)CH(Cl)CH₃ has two.


### 14. Chemistry of Hydrocarbons

**chem-14-c01 — Free-radical chlorination of methane** _(map)_

- Initiation: Cl₂ —(UV / sunlight)→ 2Cl• (homolytic; Cl–Cl is weaker than C–H)
- Propagation 1: Cl• + CH₄ → CH₃• + HCl
- Propagation 2: CH₃• + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl• (Cl• regenerated → chain)
- Termination: any two radicals join: Cl• + Cl• → Cl₂; CH₃• + Cl• → CH₃Cl; CH₃• + CH₃• → C₂H₆

Excess Cl₂ goes on: CH₃Cl → CH₂Cl₂ → CHCl₃ → CCl₄. Halogen reactivity: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂. In the dark at room temperature: no reaction.

💡 Traces of ethane (from methane) or butane (from ethane) prove CH₃• / C₂H₅• radicals joined in termination.


**chem-14-c02 — Ethene: made and reacted** _(map)_

*Making ethene (elimination)*

- C₂H₅OH —(conc. H₂SO₄, 170 °C; or Al₂O₃, 350 °C)→ CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O (at 140 °C with excess ethanol: diethyl ether)
- C₂H₅Br + KOH —(alcoholic, heat)→ CH₂=CH₂ + KBr + H₂O

*Reactions of CH₂=CH₂ (addition)*

- —(H₂, Ni, 200–300 °C)→ ethane
- —(Br₂ in CCl₄, room temp)→ 1,2-dibromoethane (red-brown colour goes)
- —(HBr)→ bromoethane; HI > HBr > HCl
- —(cold conc. H₂SO₄, then boil with water)→ ethanol
- —(cold dilute alkaline KMnO₄, Baeyer's)→ ethane-1,2-diol (pink goes)
- —(O₃, then Zn/H₂O)→ 2 HCHO (methanal)
- —(high pressure, heat, trace O₂)→ polythene

💡 Markovnikov: propene + HBr → 2-bromopropane (H goes to the carbon that already has more H).


**chem-14-c03 — Benzene: electrophilic substitution** _(table)_

| Reaction | Reagent, conditions | Electrophile | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitration | conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄, 50–55 °C | NO₂⁺ | nitrobenzene |
| Sulphonation | fuming H₂SO₄ (oleum); reversible | SO₃ | benzenesulphonic acid |
| Halogenation | Cl₂ / FeCl₃ (Br₂ / FeBr₃), dark | Cl⁺ (Br⁺) | chlorobenzene |
| F–C alkylation | CH₃Cl / anhydrous AlCl₃ | CH₃⁺ | methylbenzene |
| F–C acylation | CH₃COCl / anhydrous AlCl₃ | CH₃CO⁺ | acetophenone |

Steps: electrophile forms → attacks ring (slow) → arenium ion (one sp³ carbon) → loses H⁺ (fast), aromaticity restored.

💡 o/p directors: –CH₃, –OH, –NH₂, halogens (but halogens deactivate). Meta directors: –NO₂, –CHO, –COOH, –CN, –SO₃H.

⚠️ Above ~60 °C nitration also gives m-dinitrobenzene.


**chem-14-c04 — Ethyne: preparation, addition, acidity** _(map)_

- BrCH₂CH₂Br —(alcoholic KOH)→ CH₂=CHBr —(NaNH₂)→ HC≡CH (double dehydrohalogenation)
- Br₂CHCHBr₂ —(Zn dust, heat)→ HC≡CH
- HC≡CH —(H₂, Ni)→ ethene —(H₂, Ni)→ ethane
- HC≡CH —(HBr)→ CH₂=CHBr —(HBr)→ CH₃CHBr₂ (1,1-dibromoethane, Markovnikov)
- HC≡CH —(dil. H₂SO₄ + HgSO₄, 75 °C)→ [CH₂=CHOH, vinyl alcohol] → CH₃CHO; propyne gives propanone

*Acidity of ≡C–H*

sp carbon has 50% s character, so it holds electrons tightly: ethyne > ethene > ethane. Na → sodium acetylide + H₂; ammoniacal AgNO₃ → white ppt (Ag₂C₂); ammoniacal Cu₂Cl₂ → red ppt (Cu₂C₂).

💡 Only terminal alkynes give these precipitates: but-1-yne yes, but-2-yne no.


### 15. Alkyl Halides

**chem-15-c01 — SN2 vs SN1** _(table)_

| Feature | SN2 | SN1 |
|---|---|---|
| Steps | one, concerted | two, via carbocation |
| Rate law | k[RX][Nu⁻], 2nd order | k[RX], 1st order |
| Nucleophile attacks | from the back, 180° to X | either face of flat carbocation |
| Stereo result | inversion of configuration | mainly racemic mixture |
| Fastest for | CH₃X > 1° > 2° > 3° | 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃X |

C–X carbon is δ+ and sp³, so nucleophiles attack it. Reactivity R–I > R–Br > R–Cl > R–F: the weakest C–X bond breaks easiest, so I⁻ is the best leaving group.

⚠️ Polarity is highest for C–F, but bond strength decides reactivity, not polarity.


**chem-15-c02 — Substitution or elimination? Read the solvent** _(trick)_

- Aqueous KOH / NaOH, warm → alcohol (substitution).
- Alcoholic KOH, heat → alkene (elimination): H lost from the β-carbon, X from the α-carbon.
- E2: one step, rate = k[RX][B⁻], strong base. E1: two steps via carbocation, rate = k[RX]; same slow step as SN1.
- Elimination is favoured by a strong, concentrated base, alcoholic solvent, high temperature and a 3° halide.

(CH₃)₃CBr + hot alcoholic KOH → 2-methylpropene. 1-bromopropane and 2-bromopropane both give propene.

⚠️ No β-hydrogen, no alkene: CH₃Br and (CH₃)₃CCH₂Br cannot eliminate.


### 16. Alcohols & Phenols

**chem-16-c01 — Alcohol reaction map (ROH)** _(map)_

- ROH —Na→ RO⁻Na⁺ (alkoxide) + ½H₂ (gentler than Na + water)
- ROH —PCl₅→ RCl + POCl₃ + HCl
- ROH —SOCl₂ (pyridine)→ RCl + SO₂↑ + HCl↑
- ROH —conc. H₂SO₄, ~180 °C→ alkene + H₂O
- 2ROH —conc. H₂SO₄, ~140 °C (excess alcohol)→ ROR (ether)
- ROH + R′COOH —conc. H₂SO₄, warm⇌ R′COOR (ester) + H₂O
- ROH + R′COCl —room temp→ ester + HCl (fast, not reversible)
- RO⁻Na⁺ + R′X → ROR′ + NaX (Williamson ether synthesis)

💡 💡 SOCl₂ is preferred because both by-products are gases, leaving pure RCl.

⚠️ ⚠️ In esterification the acid loses –OH and the alcohol loses H, so ¹⁸O from the alcohol ends up in the ester, not in the water.


**chem-16-c02 — Telling 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols apart** _(table)_

| Test | 1° RCH₂OH | 2° R₂CHOH | 3° R₃COH |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lucas (conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂) | no cloudiness at room temp | cloudy in ~5 min | cloudy at once |
| K₂Cr₂O₇ / H⁺ | aldehyde → carboxylic acid | ketone | no reaction |
| Colour change | orange → green | orange → green | stays orange |

Example: propan-1-ol → propanal → propanoic acid; propan-2-ol → propanone; 2-methylpropan-2-ol is not oxidised.

💡 💡 3° alcohols resist oxidation because the C bearing –OH has no H atom to lose.

⚠️ ⚠️ Iodoform (I₂ + NaOH → yellow CHI₃) is given by ethanol and by alcohols with CH₃CH(OH)– , not by methanol or propan-1-ol.


**chem-16-c03 — Phenol vs ethanol, and phenol's ring reactions** _(table)_

| Test | Phenol | Ethanol |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral FeCl₃ | violet colour | no colour |
| Bromine water | decolourised, white ppt | no change |
| Aqueous NaOH | dissolves (sodium phenoxide) | no reaction |
| NaHCO₃ | no CO₂ | no CO₂ |
| Sodium metal | H₂ | H₂ |
| I₂ + NaOH (iodoform) | no | yellow CHI₃ |

- Br₂ water → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (3 Br₂ per phenol)
- Br₂ in CS₂, low temp → 2- and 4-bromophenol
- Dilute HNO₃ → 2- and 4-nitrophenol

### 17. Aldehydes & Ketones

**chem-17-c01 — Making aldehydes and ketones** _(map)_

- 1° alcohol —K₂Cr₂O₇ / dil. H₂SO₄, distil off at once→ aldehyde
- 2° alcohol —K₂Cr₂O₇ / dil. H₂SO₄→ ketone
- Alkene —O₃, then Zn + H₂O→ aldehydes and/or ketones
- Methanol vapour + air —hot Ag (or Cu) catalyst, ~500 °C→ methanal (industry)
- Ethene + air —PdCl₂ / CuCl₂ in water→ ethanal (industry)

Ozonolysis examples: propene → methanal + ethanal; 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene → propanone only.

💡 💡 Distilling the aldehyde off stops it being oxidised further to the carboxylic acid.

⚠️ ⚠️ Zn in ozonolysis removes H₂O₂, so the aldehyde is not oxidised to an acid.


**chem-17-c02 — Nucleophilic addition reactions of C=O** _(map)_

- + HCN (trace base, makes CN⁻) → cyanohydrin; then H₃O⁺, boil → 2-hydroxy acid
- + saturated NaHSO₃ → crystalline bisulphite adduct (aldehydes, methyl ketones)
- + 2,4-DNPH → orange/yellow 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone (addition then loss of H₂O)
- + NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄ (H⁻), or H₂/Ni → aldehyde gives 1° alcohol, ketone gives 2° alcohol
- Dilute NaOH, needs α-H → aldol (ethanal → 3-hydroxybutanal); heat → but-2-enal
- Conc. NaOH, no α-H (HCHO, C₆H₅CHO) → Cannizzaro: alcohol + carboxylate salt

Reactivity: HCHO > CH₃CHO > CH₃COCH₃. Carbon goes sp² → sp³; nucleophile adds to C, H to O.

⚠️ ⚠️ LiAlH₄ reacts violently with water, so it is used in dry ether; NaBH₄ works in water or alcohol.


**chem-17-c03 — Carbonyl identification tests** _(table)_

| Reagent | Positive result | Given by |
|---|---|---|
| 2,4-DNPH (Brady's) | orange/yellow ppt | all aldehydes and ketones |
| Tollens' (ammoniacal AgNO₃) | silver mirror | aldehydes only (incl. benzaldehyde) |
| Fehling's (CuSO₄ + alkaline Rochelle salt) | brick-red Cu₂O | aliphatic aldehydes only |
| I₂ + NaOH (iodoform) | yellow CHI₃, antiseptic smell | CH₃CO– compounds, e.g. ethanal, propanone |

Tollens': aldehyde is oxidised to the acid (as its salt); Ag⁺ is reduced to Ag. 1 mol RCHO deposits 2 mol Ag (methanal, HCHO, can give 4 mol).

💡 💡 Benzaldehyde: Tollens' positive, Fehling's negative. Propanal vs propanone: Tollens' or Fehling's. Ethanal vs propanal: iodoform.

⚠️ ⚠️ Methanal gives no iodoform. Strong oxidants split ketones into shorter acids.


### 18. Carboxylic Acids

**chem-18-c01 — Carboxylic acid map: make it, change it** _(map)_

*Making RCOOH*

- RCH₂OH —K₂Cr₂O₇ / H⁺, reflux→ RCOOH
- RX —KCN (ethanol)→ RCN —H₃O⁺, heat→ RCOOH (one C longer)
- RMgX —CO₂, then H₃O⁺→ RCOOH (one C longer)

*Reactions of RCOOH*

- —SOCl₂→ RCOCl + SO₂↑ + HCl↑ ; —PCl₅→ RCOCl + POCl₃ + HCl

**chem-18-c02 — Acid strength and derivative reactivity orders** _(trick)_

*Acid strength*

Cl₃CCOOH > Cl₂CHCOOH > ClCH₂COOH > HCOOH > CH₃COOH > C₆H₅OH > H₂O > C₂H₅OH

- RCOO⁻ is resonance-stabilised: charge shared equally over two O atoms
- Electron-withdrawing Cl (–I) strengthens the acid; more Cl, stronger
- Alkyl groups (+I) weaken it, so HCOOH > CH₃COOH

*Reactivity towards nucleophiles (e.g. water)*

acyl chloride > anhydride > ester > amide

💡 💡 Acyl chlorides fume in moist air (hydrolysis gives HCl) and give amides with NH₃ at room temperature.

⚠️ ⚠️ Ethanoic acid is weak: only partly ionised, so it conducts less than HCl of the same concentration.

**chem-19-c01 — Four levels of protein structure** _(table)_

| Level | What it is | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | sequence of amino acids | peptide bonds (–CO–NH–) |
| Secondary | α-helix or β-pleated sheet | H-bonds between backbone C=O and N–H |
| Tertiary | 3-D folding of one chain | H-bonds, ionic, disulphide (–S–S–), hydrophobic |
| Quaternary | two or more chains together | same forces, e.g. haemoglobin (4 chains) |

- Peptide bond formation is condensation: H₂O is lost
- n amino acids in one chain → n − 1 peptide bonds and n − 1 H₂O
- Conjugated protein = protein + prosthetic group (haemoglobin: haem)
- Fibrous (keratin, collagen) insoluble; globular (enzymes, haemoglobin) soluble

⚠️ ⚠️ Denaturation (heat, acid, alcohol) destroys 2°, 3°, 4° structure; the primary structure stays.

### 2. Force & Motion

**phy-02-c01 — Equations of motion and graphs** _(formula)_

- v = u + at
- s = ut + ½at²
- v² = u² + 2as
- s = ½(u + v)t  (average velocity × time)

| Graph | Slope gives | Area gives |
|---|---|---|
| displacement–time | velocity | — |

💡 From rest with uniform a, distances in the 1st, 2nd, 3rd second are in the ratio 1 : 3 : 5.

**phy-02-c02 — Projectile formula sheet** _(formula)_

Launch speed v (m s⁻¹) at angle θ, level ground, no air resistance, g = 9.8 (or 10) m s⁻².

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Horizontal velocity | vx = v cos θ (constant) |
| Vertical velocity | vy = v sin θ − gt |

**phy-02-c03 — Newton's second law in common set-ups** _(table)_

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Lift accelerating up at a | scale reads m(g + a) |
| Lift accelerating down at a | scale reads m(g − a) |
| Lift at constant velocity | scale reads mg |
| Lift in free fall | scale reads 0 (weightless) |

💡 Constant velocity ⇒ net force zero (first law), even with the engine running.

⚠️ Action and reaction never cancel: they act on different bodies.


**phy-02-c04 — Momentum, impulse and collision shortcuts** _(trick)_

- p = mv; unit kg m s⁻¹ = N s. F = Δp / Δt.
- Impulse = FΔt = Δp = area under the F–t graph.
- Ball rebounding at the same speed: Δp = 2mv.
- Recoil / explosion from rest: total p stays 0, so MV = mv. Speeds ∝ 1/mass; the lighter piece also gets more KE.
- Elastic: speed of approach = speed of separation (u₁ − u₂ = v₂ − v₁); KE conserved.
- Equal masses, one at rest, elastic: velocities swap.
- Very heavy hits very light at rest: light one moves off at 2u.
- Perfectly inelastic: bodies stick; largest KE loss.

⚠️ Momentum is conserved in every collision with no external force; KE is conserved only in elastic ones.

**phy-03-c02 — Kinetic energy and momentum links** _(trick)_

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| speed doubled | K × 4, p × 2 |
| K up by 300% (× 4) | p × 2, i.e. up by 100% |
| equal K, masses m and 4m | p ratio 1 : 2 (p ∝ √m) |
| equal p | K ∝ 1/m (lighter body has more K) |

⚠️ "Increases by 300%" means 4 times, not 3 times. K can never be negative, since v² ≥ 0.

### 4. Rotational & Circular Motion

**phy-04-c01 — Angle units and angular velocity** _(formula)_

- θ = s / r  (arc length ÷ radius), in radians.
- 1 rev = 2π rad = 360°; 1 rad ≈ 57.3°; 1° = π/180 rad.
- ω = Δθ / Δt = 2πf = 2π / T  (rad s⁻¹, dimensions [T⁻¹]).
- rev per minute N → ω = 2πN / 60.  120 rpm = 4π rad s⁻¹, T = 0.5 s.
- Earth: ω = 2π / (24 h) ≈ 7.3 × 10⁻⁵ rad s⁻¹.

Direction of ω (and of a small angular displacement) is along the axis, by the right-hand rule: anticlockwise seen from above → upward.

💡 Minute hand : hour hand angular velocity = 12 : 1. In 20 min the minute hand turns 120° = 2π/3 rad.

⚠️ A radian is a ratio of two lengths, so it has no dimensions. One revolution is 2π rad for any radius.


**phy-04-c02 — Linear ↔ angular relations** _(table)_

| Linear | Angular | Link |
|---|---|---|
| arc length s (m) | θ (rad) | s = rθ |
| speed v (m s⁻¹) | ω (rad s⁻¹) | v = rω |
| tangential a (m s⁻²) | α (rad s⁻²) | a = rα |

- Rigid body: every point has the same θ, ω, α; v grows with r (v₁ : v₂ = r₁ : r₂).
- Belt or gears without slipping: rims share the same v, so ω ∝ 1/r.
- Rolling without slipping: distance = n × 2πr; ω = v / r.
- Linear velocity is along the tangent to the circle.

⚠️ Use θ in radians in these links. Convert degrees or revolutions first.


### 5. Fluid Dynamics

**phy-05-c01 — Drag and terminal velocity** _(formula)_

| Formula | Symbols | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| F = 6πηrv (Stokes' law) | η viscosity, r radius, v speed | F in N; η in Pa s = N s m⁻² = kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹ |
| v_t = 2ρgr²/9η (no upthrust) | ρ density of sphere, g gravity | m s⁻¹ |

- Drag is a resistive force from the fluid, always opposite to the motion (down for a ball thrown up).
- At terminal velocity: weight = drag (+ upthrust), net force 0, acceleration 0.
- v–t graph: curve rising, then flat line. Acceleration falls from g to zero.

💡 v_t ∝ r²: double the radius → 4× terminal speed. v_t ∝ 1/η: double viscosity → half speed.

⚠️ Stokes' law needs a small sphere moving slowly (laminar flow). It fails at high speed, where flow turns turbulent.


**phy-05-c02 — Ideal fluid: laminar vs turbulent** _(table)_

An ideal fluid is incompressible (density constant), non-viscous (no internal friction) and flows steadily.

| Feature | Laminar (streamline) | Turbulent |
|---|---|---|
| Velocity at a fixed point | Constant in time | Changes erratically |
| Layers | Slide smoothly, never mix | Mix, form eddies and swirls |
| Streamlines | Never cross | Not defined |
| When | Low speed, narrow pipe, high viscosity | High speed, wide pipe, low viscosity |
| Energy loss | Low | High |
| Example | Tap opened slightly | Tap fully open, rivers, wind |

💡 Tangent to a streamline gives the velocity direction. Streamlines closer together = faster flow.

⚠️ Bernoulli's equation is exact only for an ideal fluid in steady (streamline) flow; Stokes' law needs slow, laminar flow round a small sphere.


**phy-05-c03 — Continuity and Bernoulli's equation** _(formula)_

| Equation | Meaning | Conserves |
|---|---|---|
| A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ | A cross-section area (m²), v speed (m s⁻¹); Av = volume flow rate (m³ s⁻¹) | Mass |
| P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant | P pressure (Pa), ρ density, h height | Energy (per unit volume) |

- Narrow part → faster flow → lower pressure (Bernoulli effect).
- Horizontal tube: P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂².
- Uses: aeroplane wing lift, Venturi meter, spray gun, chimney draught.
- Blood flow: at a narrowed (stenosed) artery, blood speeds up and its pressure drops, so the artery can be squeezed shut by outside pressure.

⚠️ Speed depends on area, so v ∝ 1/r². Half the radius → 4× the speed, not 2×.


### 6. Waves

**phy-06-c01 — Wave types and v = fλ** _(table)_

|---|---|---|
| Particles vibrate | ⊥ to wave direction | ∥ to wave direction |
| Made of | Crests and troughs | Compressions and rarefactions |
| λ = distance between | Two adjacent crests | Two adjacent compressions |
| Examples | Rope, light, all EM waves | Sound, slinky pushed along |

**phy-06-c02 — Speed of sound: Newton and Laplace** _(formula)_

| Formula | Idea | Value in air at 0 °C |
|---|---|---|
| v = √(P/ρ) (Newton) | Assumed isothermal compressions | ≈ 280 m s⁻¹ (too low) |
| v = √(γP/ρ) (Laplace) | Compressions are adiabatic; γ = Cp/Cv = 1.4 for air | ≈ 332 m s⁻¹ (matches) |

- v ∝ √T (T in kelvin): v_t ≈ v₀ + 0.61t m s⁻¹, t in °C.
- Pressure change at constant temperature: no effect on v.
- Moist air is less dense, so sound is faster.
- Speed: solids > liquids > gases.

⚠️ Use kelvin in v ∝ √T. Going from 27 °C to 927 °C is 300 K → 1200 K, so v doubles.


**phy-06-c03 — Stationary waves: strings and pipes** _(formula)_

Two identical waves moving in opposite directions superpose to give a stationary wave. Node = zero displacement; antinode = maximum. Node to next node = λ/2; node to next antinode = λ/4.

| System | Frequencies | Harmonics |
|---|---|---|
| String fixed at both ends | fₙ = nv/2l, v = √(T/μ) | All: 1, 2, 3 … |
| Pipe open at both ends | fₙ = nv/2l | All: 1, 2, 3 … |
| Pipe closed at one end | fₙ = nv/4l | Odd only: 1, 3, 5 … |

- T tension (N), μ mass per unit length (kg m⁻¹), l length (m).
- String ends and closed pipe ends are nodes; open ends are antinodes.

💡 Same length: a closed pipe's fundamental is half an open pipe's.


**phy-06-c04 — Simple harmonic motion** _(formula)_

SHM: acceleration ∝ displacement and always towards the mean position: a = −ω²x.

| Quantity | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Mass on spring | T = 2π√(m/k) | s (k in N m⁻¹) |
| Simple pendulum | T = 2π√(l/g) | s |
| Angular frequency | ω = 2πf = 2π/T | rad s⁻¹ |
| Speed at x | v = ω√(x₀² − x²) | m s⁻¹ |

- Mean position: speed max (ωx₀), acceleration 0.
- Extremes: speed 0, acceleration max (ω²x₀).

### 7. Thermodynamics

**phy-07-c01 — Specific heats and work by a gas** _(formula)_

| Formula | Symbols | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| Q = mcΔT | c specific heat (per kg) | c: J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ |
| Q = nCΔT | C molar specific heat (per mole) | C: J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ |
| C = Mc | M molar mass in kg mol⁻¹ | — |
| Heat capacity = mc | whole body | J K⁻¹ |
| W = PΔV | P constant pressure, ΔV volume change | J (Pa m³ = J) |

- Heat flows on its own from higher to lower temperature, until thermal equilibrium (same temperature).
- Work = area under the P–V graph. Expansion: W by gas positive; compression: negative; rigid container: zero.

⚠️ Specific heat is a property of the material: doubling the mass doubles heat capacity, not c. Convert litres: 1 L = 10⁻³ m³.


**phy-07-c02 — First law: Q = ΔU + W** _(table)_

FSc convention: Q = heat supplied to the gas, W = work done BY the gas. The first law is conservation of energy.

| Process | Kept fixed | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Isochoric | Volume | W = 0, so Q = ΔU |
| Isobaric | Pressure | W = PΔV, Q = ΔU + PΔV |
| Isothermal | Temperature | ΔU = 0, so Q = W |
| Adiabatic | No heat flow | Q = 0, so W = −ΔU (expansion cools) |

- Heat given out → Q negative. Work done ON gas → W negative.
- Ideal gas: U depends only on temperature, and ΔU is path-independent.

💡 500 J in, 200 J of work out → ΔU = 500 − 200 = +300 J.

⚠️ Some books write ΔU = Q + W with W done ON the gas. Check which W the question uses.


**phy-07-c03 — Cp − Cv = R and γ** _(trick)_

Why Cp > Cv: at constant volume all heat raises U. At constant pressure the gas also expands and does work PΔV = nRΔT, so extra heat is needed.

- Cp − Cv = R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ (molar values, ideal gas).
- γ = Cp/Cv, always > 1.
- Monatomic gas (He, Ne): Cv = 3R/2, Cp = 5R/2, γ = 5/3.
- Diatomic gas (air, O₂, N₂): Cv = 5R/2, Cp = 7R/2, γ = 7/5 = 1.4.

💡 Heat at constant pressure: Q = nCpΔT. Work part = nRΔT, e.g. 2 mol, ΔT 50 K → W = 2 × 8.3 × 50 = 830 J.

⚠️ Cp − Cv = R holds for molar heats only. For specific heats (per kg): cp − cv = R/M.


### 8. Electrostatics

**phy-08-c01 — Coulomb's law and electric field** _(formula)_

| Formula | Symbols | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| F = kq₁q₂/r² | k = 1/4πε₀ = 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻² | N |
| F_medium = F/εr | εr relative permittivity (≥ 1, no unit) | N |
| E = F/q₀ | q₀ small positive test charge | N C⁻¹ = V m⁻¹ |

**phy-08-c02 — Zero-field points, field lines, plates** _(trick)_

*Where is E = 0 on the line?*

- Like charges: between them, nearer the smaller one. +q and +4q, 30 cm apart → 10 cm from +q.
- Unlike, unequal charges: outside, beyond the smaller one.
- Equal like charges: midpoint field = 0. Equal unlike, separation d: midpoint field = 8kq/d², pointing to −q.

*Field lines*

- Start on +, end on −; never cross; closer lines = stronger field.
- Tangent gives the direction of E.

| Charged surface | Field |
|---|---|
| Thin infinite sheet | σ/2ε₀ |
| Just outside a conducting plate | σ/ε₀ |
| Between plates +σ and −σ | σ/ε₀ (outside: 0) |

💡 A plate's field is uniform: it does not change with distance.


**phy-08-c03 — Potential and RC charging** _(formula)_

| Formula | Meaning | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| V = W/q | work per unit + charge from infinity | V = J C⁻¹ |
| V = kq/r | potential of a point charge (scalar) | V |
| E = ΔV/d | uniform field between plates | V m⁻¹ |
| τ = RC | time constant | s (Ω × F) |
| q = q₀(1 − e^(−t/RC)) | charging | C |
| q = q₀e^(−t/RC) | discharging | C |

- 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J (a unit of energy). 1 V = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹.
- After one τ: charging reaches 63%; discharging falls to 37%.

⚠️ V ∝ 1/r but E ∝ 1/r². No work is done moving a charge between points at the same potential.


### 9. Current Electricity

**phy-09-c01 — Current, Ohm's law and resistivity** _(formula)_

| Formula | Symbols | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| I = Q/t | charge Q passing in time t | A = C s⁻¹ |
| n = I/e | electrons passing per second | s⁻¹ |
| V = IR | p.d. V, current I, resistance R | Ω = V A⁻¹ |
| R = ρL/A | resistivity ρ, length L, cross-section area A | ρ in Ω m |

Ohm's law holds only if temperature and other physical conditions stay constant. Filament lamps, diodes and thermistors are non-ohmic.

⚠️ Stretch a wire to n × its length (same volume): A falls n times, so R becomes n²R. Double the diameter: A is 4×, so R becomes R/4. ρ depends only on material and temperature, not on shape.


**phy-09-c02 — Resistance and temperature** _(table)_

R_T = R₀(1 + αΔT), so α = (R_T − R₀)/(R₀ΔT). Unit of α: K⁻¹ (per °C is the same size).

| Material | Sign of α | Why / use |
|---|---|---|
| Metals (copper, tungsten) | positive | atoms vibrate more, electrons collide more |
| Carbon, semiconductors, thermistors | negative | more free charge carriers when hot |
| Manganin, constantan (alloys) | very small | standard resistors: R hardly changes |

💡 Worked check: R₀ = 10 Ω, α = 4 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹, ΔT = 50 K → R = 10(1 + 0.2) = 12 Ω.


**phy-09-c03 — emf, internal resistance, maximum power** _(formula)_

- I = E/(R + r): emf E, load R, internal resistance r
- Terminal p.d. V = E − Ir (lost volts = Ir)
- Open circuit (I = 0): V = E. Short circuit: I = E/r
- V–I graph: straight line, y-intercept = E, slope = −r
- Power wasted inside the cell = I²r

*Maximum power transfer*

- Happens when R = r
- P_max = E²/4r, terminal p.d. = E/2, efficiency = 50%

⚠️ Maximum power is not maximum efficiency. As R rises from 0, power to R rises, peaks at R = r, then falls.

**phy-10-c02 — Charged particle in a magnetic field** _(trick)_

| Motion relative to B | Force | Path |
|---|---|---|
| at rest or along B | zero | rest / straight line |

### 11. Electromagnetic Induction

**phy-11-c01 — Faraday's and Lenz's laws** _(formula)_

- ε = −N ΔΦ/Δt: N turns, ΔΦ change of flux (Wb) per turn, Δt time (s)
- 1 Wb s⁻¹ = 1 V
- Moving rod: ε = BLv (B, L and v mutually ⊥)
- Steady flux (magnet at rest) → no emf. Faster change → bigger emf

Lenz: the induced current opposes the change that causes it (the minus sign). This is conservation of energy: you must do work to push the magnet.

💡 N pole approaching → near end of coil becomes N (repels). N pole leaving → near end becomes S (attracts back). A cut ring carries no current, so a falling magnet accelerates at g.


**phy-11-c02 — Transformer and power transmission** _(formula)_

Works by mutual induction, so it needs AC; steady DC gives no changing flux. Frequency is unchanged.

Ideal: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np = Ip/Is (power in = power out).

| Feature | Reason |
|---|---|
| Soft iron core | easily magnetised, low hysteresis loss |
| Laminated core | cuts eddy-current loss |
| Thick wire on low-V side | carries high current, less I²R heat |

Step-up at power station, step-down near homes (to about 220 V). Line loss P = I²R with I = P/V.

⚠️ Raise V by 10× → I falls 10× → loss falls 100×. E.g. 2 MW at 200 kV: I = 10 A, loss in 10 Ω = 1 kW.


### 12. Alternating Current

**phy-12-c01 — R, L and C in AC circuits** _(table)_

> **CIVIL**
> - C-I-V: in a Capacitor, I leads V by 90°
> - V-I-L: in an Inductor (L), V leads I by 90° (I lags)

| Element | Opposition | As f rises | DC (f = 0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resistor | R, in phase | no change | R |
| Inductor | X_L = 2πfL | rises (∝ f) | zero opposition |
| Capacitor | X_C = 1/(2πfC) | falls (∝ 1/f) | blocks once charged |

- Reactance unit: Ω. Average power in pure L or C over a cycle = 0
- 90° = a quarter period: 5 ms at 50 Hz. In series RLC, V_L and V_C are 180° apart

💡 L = (1/π) H at 50 Hz → X_L = 100 Ω.


**phy-12-c02 — Electromagnetic spectrum** _(table)_

| Wave (λ decreasing ↓) | Typical λ | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Radio | m to km | broadcasting |
| Microwave | mm to cm | radar, cooking, phones |
| Infrared | ≈ 700 nm to 1 mm | remote controls, heat imaging, optical-fibre communication |
| Visible | 400–700 nm | seeing, endoscopes (fibre optics) |
| Ultraviolet | ≈ 10–400 nm | sterilising, vitamin D |
| X-rays | ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m | imaging bones |

All travel at c = 3 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ in vacuum; c = fλ. Gamma: highest f and energy.

💡 1 MHz radio → λ = 300 m. 3 cm microwave → f = 10¹⁰ Hz. UV lies just past violet.


### 13. Electronics

**phy-13-c01 — Half-wave vs full-wave rectifiers** _(table)_

Rectification = changing AC into DC (current in one direction only).

|  | Half-wave | Full-wave (centre-tap) | Bridge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diodes | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| Conducting per half-cycle | 1 (other half: none) | 1 | 2 |
| Halves used | positive only | both | both |
| Pulses/s from 50 Hz AC | 50 | 100 | 100 |
| Special need | — | centre-tapped transformer | no centre tap |

In full-wave, load current flows in the same direction in both halves. Output without a filter is pulsating DC; a capacitor smooths it.

💡 Full-wave output frequency = 2 × input frequency. 200 pulses/s means 100 Hz input.


**phy-13-c02 — PN junction: forward vs reverse bias** _(table)_

Depletion region: no free carriers, only fixed ions — negative ions on the p-side, positive ions on the n-side. Barrier: Si ≈ 0.7 V, Ge ≈ 0.3 V.

|  | Forward bias | Reverse bias |
|---|---|---|
| Connection | p to +, n to − | p to −, n to + |
| Depletion region | narrows | widens |
| Barrier | lowered | raised |
| Current | large (majority carriers) | tiny leakage (minority carriers) |
| Ideal diode acts as | closed switch | open switch |

Majority carriers: holes in p-type, electrons in n-type.

⚠️ A Si diode conducts only above 0.7 V. With 0.4 V forward, current ≈ 0. In a circuit: I = (V − 0.7)/R, e.g. (5.7 − 0.7)/1 kΩ = 5 mA.


### 14. Dawn of Modern Physics

**phy-14-c01 — Photon energy** _(formula)_

- E = hf = hc/λ
- E = photon energy (J); f = frequency (Hz); λ = wavelength (m)
- h = Planck's constant ≈ 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s (unit J s); c = 3 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹
- 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J (divide J by 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ to get eV)
- Power P = n·hf, so photons per second n = P/hf

Light comes in packets (photons). Higher f / shorter λ → more energy per photon: violet > red, X-ray > UV > visible > IR.

⚠️ E ∝ 1/λ, not λ. Halve the wavelength → energy doubles. Brighter light means more photons, not more energetic photons.


**phy-14-c02 — Photoelectric effect** _(formula)_

- hf = φ + K.E.max (Einstein's equation)
- φ = work function = hf₀ = minimum energy to free an electron (J or eV)
- f₀ = threshold frequency: below it, no electrons at any intensity
- K.E.max = ½mv²max = eV₀ (V₀ = stopping potential)
- Graph K.E.max vs f: straight line, slope = h, x-intercept = f₀

| Increase… | Number of electrons | K.E.max |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity (f > f₀) | increases | no change |
| Frequency | no change | increases |

💡 Quick sum: 5 eV photon on a 2 eV metal → K.E.max = 3 eV. The effect proves light behaves as particles.


### 15. Atomic Spectra

**phy-15-c01 — Energy levels and emitted photons** _(formula)_

- Hydrogen: Eₙ = −13.6/n² eV (n = 1, 2, 3 …) → −13.6, −3.4, −1.51 eV
- Emission: hf = E_upper − E_lower = ΔE
- λ = hc/ΔE (bigger gap → shorter λ)
- Ionisation from ground state = 13.6 eV
- Lines possible from level n down to ground: n(n − 1)/2

Levels are fixed (quantised), so only certain photon energies appear → line spectrum, unique for each element. Negative energy means the electron is bound (zero = free).

💡 −3.4 → −13.6 eV gives a 10.2 eV photon. An atom absorbs only photons that exactly match a gap (e.g. 10.2 eV or 12.09 eV from ground).


**phy-15-c02 — Hydrogen spectral series** _(mnemonic)_

> **Lazy Boys Play Basketball Peacefully**
> - Lazy = Lyman → n = 1, ultraviolet
> - Boys = Balmer → n = 2, visible
> - Play = Paschen → n = 3, infrared
> - Basketball = Brackett → n = 4, infrared
> - Peacefully = Pfund → n = 5, infrared

The series is named by the LOWER level the electron falls to. The first Balmer line (red, 656 nm) is n = 3 → 2. n = 5 → 3 is Paschen.

⚠️ Longest λ in a series = smallest jump (Lyman: 2 → 1). Shortest λ (highest energy) in a series = series limit, ∞ → lower level (Lyman: ∞ → 1, 13.6 eV).


### 16. Nuclear Physics

**phy-16-c01 — Alpha, beta and gamma** _(table)_

|  | α | β | γ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | helium nucleus ⁴₂He | fast electron | EM wave (photon) |
| Change in A, Z | A − 4, Z − 2 | A same, Z + 1 | no change |
| Stopped by | paper | few mm aluminium | thick lead (reduced only) |
| Ionising power | highest | medium | lowest |
| In E or B field | deflected | deflected (opposite, more) | undeflected |
| Medical use | not used as an external beam (cannot pass skin) | I-131 thyroid therapy | Co-60 radiotherapy, Tc-99m tracer, sterilising |

Nucleus = protons (Z) + neutrons (A − Z). Isotopes: same Z, different neutrons, same chemistry.

💡 Decay chains: no. of α = ΔA/4; no. of β = 2α − ΔZ. Th-232 → Pb-208: α = 6, β = 4.


**phy-16-c02 — Half-life and decay constant** _(formula)_

- λ = 0.693/T½ (λ = decay constant, unit s⁻¹; T½ = half-life, s)
- N = N₀ (½)ⁿ, where n = t/T½ = number of half-lives
- Activity A = λN, unit becquerel (1 Bq = 1 decay s⁻¹)
- Absorbed dose: gray (1 Gy = 1 J kg⁻¹)

Decay is random (can't predict which nucleus decays next) and spontaneous (rate not changed by heat, pressure or chemistry).

💡 Count halvings: 1 → ½ → ¼ → ⅛ → 1/16. 64 mg, T½ = 2 h, after 8 h → 4 mg. 15/16 decayed = 4 half-lives.

⚠️ Half-life does NOT depend on the amount of sample. Double the mass → same T½.


## English (13 cards)

### 1. Reading & Thinking Skills

**eng-01-c01 — Figures of speech at a glance** _(table)_

| Figure | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simile | compares two unlike things using 'like' or 'as' | "as quiet as a graveyard" |
| Metaphor | says one thing is another, with no 'like' or 'as' | "Time is a thief." |
| Personification | gives human actions or feelings to a thing | "The wind whispered." |
| Alliteration | repeats the same first consonant sound | "silent snow slipped slowly" |
| Hyperbole | deliberate, obvious exaggeration | "I've told you a thousand times." |
| Onomatopoeia | a word that imitates a sound | buzz, sizzle, crash |
| Oxymoron | two opposite words side by side | "a deafening silence" |

💡 Two UNLIKE things compared with 'like' or 'as' → simile; the same comparison without them → metaphor.

⚠️ "Ali is as tall as his brother" is NOT a simile: it compares two things of the same kind, literally.


**eng-01-c02 — Context clues and scanning** _(trick)_

Guess a hard word from the words around it. Look for the clue type:

- Contrast (unlike, but, although): the word means the opposite of the clue. "Unlike his talkative brother, Bilal was reticent" → reticent = quiet.
- Restatement (that is, or, a dash or commas): the meaning is given right after.
- Example (such as, for example): the examples show the meaning.
- Cause and effect (so … that, because): the result explains the word.

*Scanning for facts*

Pick the key word or number from the question, find it in the passage, then read that sentence and the one around it.

⚠️ An option that is true in real life but not stated in the passage is wrong. Answer only from the passage.


### 2. Formal & Lexical Aspects of Language

**eng-02-c01 — Irony, sarcasm, satire, parody** _(table)_

| Term | What it is | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Irony | the result or meaning is the opposite of what is expected or said | a situation |
| Sarcasm | bitter, mocking irony meant to hurt: "Oh, brilliant work!" to someone who broke a glass | a person |
| Satire | uses humour and exaggeration to expose the faults of society or rulers, hoping for change | society, institutions |
| Parody | a comic imitation of a particular writer, work or style | a work or style |

💡 A column saying roads have "so many potholes that people swim in them" mocks the city government → satire.

💡 Shades of meaning: frugal (careful, positive) vs stingy (mean, negative). Check whether the context praises or blames.


**eng-02-c02 — Tense traps and conditionals** _(rule)_

- Since (point of time) / for (period) → present perfect. ✓ "We have known each other since 2015." ✗ "We know each other since 2015."
- Interrupted action → past continuous + simple past. ✓ "While Sara was cooking, the lights went out." ✗ "While Sara cooked, the lights were going out."
- After when / if / as soon as with future meaning, use the present. ✓ "I will call you when he arrives." ✗ "… when he will arrive."

| Type | If-clause | Main clause |
|---|---|---|
| 1 real future | present: If it rains, | will + verb: we will stay in. |
| 2 unreal present | past: If I were a bird, | would + verb: I would fly. |
| 3 unreal past | had + V3: If I had known, | would have + V3: I would have studied. |

⚠️ Formal English uses 'were' for every person in type 2: ✓ "If I were" ✗ "If I would be".


**eng-02-c03 — Infinitive or gerund?** _(rule)_

- No 'to' after make, let, had better, would rather and modals. ✓ "The teacher made us rewrite the essay." ✗ "made us to rewrite"
- Passive brings 'to' back. ✓ "We were made to rewrite the essay."
- -ing after enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, keep, suggest. ✓ "Sara enjoys reading novels." ✗ "enjoys to read"
- When 'to' is a preposition, use -ing: look forward to, be used to, object to, with a view to. ✓ "I look forward to meeting you." ✗ "look forward to meet you"

💡 A gerund is an -ing word working as a noun: "Swimming is good exercise." In "He is swimming" it is part of the verb, not a gerund.

⚠️ Meaning changes: "He stopped smoking" (gave it up) vs "He stopped to smoke" (paused in order to smoke).


**eng-02-c04 — Prepositions of time and place** _(table)_

| Use | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| clock time, night | at | at 7 o'clock, at night |
| days and dates | on | on Monday, on 14 August |
| months, years, parts of the day | in | in 1947, in the morning |
| deadline: not later than | by | Submit it by Friday. |
| continuing up to a time | until | Wait until Friday. |
| position inside | in | Sara was walking in the garden. |
| movement to the inside | into | Sara walked into the garden. |

⚠️ "walking in the garden" = already there, moving about; "walked into the garden" = entered it from outside.


**eng-02-c05 — Inversion after negative openers** _(rule)_

When a negative or limiting word starts the sentence, use question order: helping verb + subject.

- ✓ "Never have I seen such rain." ✗ "Never I have seen such rain."
- Fixed pairs: Hardly / Scarcely … when; No sooner … than. ✓ "No sooner had the train left than it started raining." ✗ "No sooner had the train left when …"
- ✓ "Little did he know that his friends were planning a surprise." ✗ "Little he knew …"
- If dropped: ✓ "Had I known about the test, I would have studied harder." = If I had known …

💡 No inversion when the word is not first: "He had hardly sat down when the phone rang."


**eng-02-c06 — Passive voice and reported speech** _(table)_

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Ali repairs the fan. | The fan is repaired by Ali. |
| Ali is repairing the fan. | The fan is being repaired by Ali. |
| Ali has repaired the fan. | The fan has been repaired by Ali. |
| Did Hamza break the glass? | Was the glass broken by Hamza? |
| The principal gave Bilal a prize. | Bilal was given a prize by the principal. |

*Reported speech (past reporting verb)*

- Tense moves one step back: is → was, has → had, will → would.
- Question → asked if / whether + statement order, no question mark: Ali asked me if I was coming to the party.
- Request or order → asked / told + to-infinitive: Fatima asked Ali to lend her his pen.

⚠️ ✗ "Ali asked me was I coming." Reported questions never keep question order.


**eng-02-c07 — Pronoun agreement and adverb position** _(rule)_

- Two subjects joined by 'and' → plural pronoun. ✓ "Ayesha and Sara finished their homework." ✗ "… finished her homework."
- Each / every + noun → singular pronoun and verb. ✓ "Each of the boys has his own bag." ✗ "Each of the boys have their own bag."
- One clear antecedent only. ✗ "Ali told Bilal that he had passed." (who passed?) ✓ "Ali told Bilal, 'You have passed.'"
- who for people, which for things. ✓ "The machine which makes the ice has broken." ✗ "The machine who makes …"

*Adverb position*

- Frequency adverbs go before the main verb but after 'be' or the first helping verb. ✓ "She always arrives early." ✓ "She is always late." ✗ "She arrives always early."
- 'Only' goes next to what it limits: "Only Bilal passed the test" (nobody else) vs "Bilal passed only the English test" (no other test).


**eng-02-c08 — Transition words and punctuation** _(rule)_

| Job | Transition words |
|---|---|
| result | therefore, consequently, as a result |
| contrast | however, nevertheless, on the other hand |
| addition | moreover, furthermore, in addition |
| warning / alternative | otherwise |
| example | for example, for instance |

- Joining two full sentences with a transition: semicolon before, comma after. ✓ "Submit the form by Friday; otherwise, your admission will be cancelled." ✗ "Submit the form by Friday, otherwise your admission …"
- Plural owner: apostrophe after the s. ✓ "the two students' results" ✗ "the two student's results"
- its = belonging to it; it's = it is. ✓ "The cat hurt its paw." ✗ "The cat hurt it's paw."
- A reported question ends with a full stop. ✓ "She asked where I lived." ✗ "She asked where I lived?"


### 3. Writing Skills

**eng-03-c01 — Subject–verb agreement traps** _(rule)_

- Each / every / either / neither (of + plural) → singular verb. ✓ "Each of the students has a laptop." ✗ "Each of the students have …"
- as well as, along with, together with do not make the subject plural. ✓ "The captain, as well as his players, is happy." ✗ "… are happy."
- A sum of money, time or distance as one amount → singular. ✓ "Five hundred rupees is too much for this book."
- Neither … nor / either … or → verb agrees with the nearer subject. ✓ "Neither the teacher nor the students were late."
- The number of → singular; a number of → plural. ✓ "The number of patients has risen." ✓ "A number of patients have recovered."

💡 Cross out the words between the subject and the verb, then match.


**eng-03-c02 — Common editing errors** _(rule)_

- No preposition after discuss, reach, resemble, or enter (a place). ✓ "We discussed the plan." ✗ "We discussed about the plan."
- Cope with, not 'cope up with'. ✓ "He could not cope with the pressure." ✗ "cope up with"
- Hardly, scarcely and barely are already negative. ✓ "I could hardly hear the speaker." ✗ "I couldn't hardly hear."
- Not only … but also: put both parts before the same kind of word. ✓ "He is not only clever but also hard-working." ✗ "He not only is clever but also hard-working."
- A sentence needs a subject and a finite verb, and a 'because' clause cannot stand alone. ✗ "Because the bus was late."

⚠️ Doubles to cut: 'return back', 'repeat again', 'reason … is because'.


**eng-03-c03 — Word forms and spelling** _(trick)_

| The gap comes … | It needs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| after 'the', 'a' or 'his', with no noun after the gap | a noun | The construction of the bridge took three years. |
| before a noun | an adjective | a careful driver |
| after an action verb, telling how | an adverb | She spoke so movingly that … |
| after be, seem, feel, look (linking) | an adjective | The soup tastes good. |

- accommodation: double c, double m
- necessary: one c, double s
- receive, ceiling: i before e, except after c
- occurred, beginning: double the last consonant before -ed/-ing when the stress is at the end
- advice (noun) / advise (verb)

⚠️ ✗ "She spoke so moving that …" — the gap tells how she spoke, so it needs the adverb.


## Logical Reasoning (12 cards)

### 1. Critical Thinking

**lr-01-c01 — If–then: what must be true** _(table)_

Rule given: "If P, then Q." Only two moves are safe.

| You are told | Conclusion | Valid? |
|---|---|---|
| P happened | So Q happened | ✅ Yes |
| Q did not happen | So P did not happen | ✅ Yes |
| Q happened | So P happened | ❌ No |
| P did not happen | So Q did not happen | ❌ No |

Example: "If it is a holiday, the bank is closed. The bank is open." → It is not a holiday (row 2).

⚠️ "The bank is closed, so it is a holiday" is the classic trap (row 3): the bank may close for other reasons.

💡 In "must be true" questions, reject any option that needs outside knowledge or opinion.


**lr-01-c02 — Strong argument and hidden assumption** _(trick)_

*Strong vs weak argument*

- Strong: directly about the question, gives a real reason or fact, and matters.
- Weak: uses "always / never / only / all" without proof, attacks a person, quotes popularity, or is off the point.

*Finding the assumption*

- An assumption is what the speaker takes for granted without saying it.
- Test: deny the option. If the statement then makes no sense, it is the assumption.

Example: "Bring your admit card to the exam hall." Assumed: admit cards will be checked at the hall. Deny it ("no one checks") and the notice is pointless.

⚠️ An option can be true and still not be assumed. Pick only what the statement needs.


### 2. Letter & Symbol Series

**lr-02-c01 — Number series: the difference ladder** _(trick)_

- 1. Write the differences between terms. Constant → arithmetic.
- 2. Not constant? Take differences of the differences.
- 3. Growing fast? Try ratios (×2, ×3) or ×n then ± a number.
- 4. Near squares or cubes? Test n² ± 1, n³ ± 1.
- 5. Still stuck? Split odd and even places into two series.

Example: 5, 7, 11, 17, 25, ? → differences 2, 4, 6, 8 → next +10 → 35.

| n | n² | n³ |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 16 | 64 |
| 5 | 25 | 125 |
| 6 | 36 | 216 |
| 7 | 49 | 343 |

⚠️ Check your rule on every term, not just the first three; "which number is wrong" questions hide one bad term.


**lr-02-c02 — Letter positions: EJOTY and 27 − n** _(mnemonic)_

> **EJOTY**
> - E = 5
> - J = 10
> - O = 15
> - T = 20
> - Y = 25

Opposite letter = 27 − position (pairs add to 27: A–Z, B–Y, C–X…).

|---|---|---|
| E | 5 | V (22) |
| J | 10 | Q (17) |

### 3. Logical Deductions

**lr-03-c01 — Syllogism chains: what follows** _(table)_

| Statement 1 | Statement 2 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| All P are Q | All Q are R | All P are R |
| All P are Q | No Q is R | No P is R |
| Some P are Q | All Q are R | Some P are R |
| Some P are Q | No Q is R | Some P are not R |
| Some P are Q | Some Q are R | Nothing definite |
| All P are Q | Some Q are R | Nothing definite |

- "Some P are Q" ↔ "Some Q are P" (both ways).
- "No P is Q" ↔ "No Q is P" (both ways).
- "All P are Q" turns round only as "Some Q are P" (exam convention), never "All Q are P".

⚠️ "All P are Q" never gives "All Q are P". And "Some P are not Q" cannot be turned round.


**lr-03-c02 — Ranks, rows and directions** _(trick)_

- Rank from bottom = total − rank from top + 1.
- Total in a row = left rank + right rank − 1.
- Turn right = 90° clockwise (N → E → S → W).
- Straight-line distance: Pythagoras; watch for 3-4-5 and 6-8-10.
- Blood relations: draw the family tree; mark male and female.

Example: In a class of 35, Sara is 10th from the top → 35 − 10 + 1 = 26th from the bottom.

Example: Ali walks 6 km north, turns right, walks 8 km → 10 km north-east of the start.

⚠️ Facing south, your right hand points west. Always turn from the way the person is facing, not from north.


### 4. Logical Problems

**lr-04-c01 — Puzzles: the slot-filling method** _(trick)_

- 1. Draw the slots (seats, floors, days) and list the people.
- 2. Place fixed clues first ("Ali is in seat 2").
- 3. Then linked clues ("immediately right of", "opposite").
- 4. Then negatives ("not in seat 4") to cut the choices.
- 5. Re-read every clue against the finished grid.

Example: Seats 1–4 in a row, numbered left to right, all facing north. Ali is in seat 2. Sara is immediately right of Ali. Bilal is not in seat 4. Where is Hamza? → Sara 3; Bilal must take 1; Hamza is in seat 4.

⚠️ "Immediately right" means the very next seat; "to the right" means anywhere on that side.

💡 Round table, all facing the centre: a person's right is the anticlockwise direction.


**lr-04-c02 — Coding–decoding: five common codes** _(table)_

| Code type | Example |
|---|---|
| Shift each letter | DOG → EPH (+1) |
| Write backwards | SALT → TLAS |
| Opposite letter (27 − n) | BAD → YZW |
| Add letter positions | BED → 2 + 5 + 4 = 11 |
| Word codes | "ka lo" = red pen, "lo mi" = pen box → lo = pen |

For word codes, find the word the two sentences share; the code they share stands for it.

⚠️ Test the rule on every letter. Wrong options often get the first two letters right and the rest wrong.

💡 Decoding (code → word)? Run the rule backwards: if the code adds 1, subtract 1.


### 5. Course of Action

**lr-05-c01 — Does a course of action follow?** _(trick)_

An action follows only if it passes all three tests:

- 1. Relevant: it tackles the problem in the statement.
- 2. Practical: it can really be done by the people named.
- 3. Proportionate: not extreme, and does not punish innocent people.

Example: Patients wait hours at a hospital because only one registration counter is open. I. Open more counters at busy hours. II. Close the outpatient department. → Only I follows.

💡 An immediate fix and a step to stop it happening again can both follow → "Both I and II follow".

⚠️ Judge only from the statement. Do not reject a sensible action just because it costs money.


**lr-05-c02 — Flag words in courses of action** _(table)_

| Usually fails ❌ | Usually follows ✅ |
|---|---|
| Ban for everyone | Inspect or test |
| Close permanently / shut down | Repair or replace |
| Shift to another city | Train or add staff |
| Punish all, including the innocent | Act against those at fault |
| Ignore the problem | Warn or advise the public |

Example: A few students used phones in a test. "Ban phones in the whole city" fails; "check bags before future tests" follows.

⚠️ Flag words are a first filter, not proof. Always re-check the action against the exact problem.


### 6. Cause & Effect

**lr-06-c01 — Cause and effect: the four verdicts** _(table)_

Ask: Which came first? Can one directly produce the other? Could one third event explain both?

| Verdict | Example pair |
|---|---|
| I is the cause, II its effect | I. Snow blocked the mountain pass. II. Fruit trucks could not reach the town. |
| II is the cause, I its effect | I. The shop's fridge stopped working. II. The shop's power was cut. |
| Common cause | I. Schools in the city closed. II. Offices in the city closed. (e.g. a storm warning) |
| Both are effects of independent causes | I. A Karachi team won a match. II. A bridge opened in Swat. |

⚠️ Two events on the same day are not cause and effect unless one can really bring about the other.


**lr-06-c02 — Breaking a false belief** _(trick)_

Superstitions and weak cause claims usually rest on one of these:

- Coincidence: two things happened together once or twice.
- Selective memory: only the matching cases are remembered.
- Hidden third factor: hot weather raises both ice-cream sales and fan sales.
- No mechanism: nothing links the "cause" to the result.

Example: Umar thinks his green cap makes his cricket team win. Best answer: a cap worn by a spectator has no way to change how the players play.

⚠️ An answer that mocks the believer ("only foolish people think so") is weak: it attacks the person, not the claim.

💡 For positive thinking, pick the option showing effort changes the result.
