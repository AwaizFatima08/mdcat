D) Becomes four times as long  
_Half-life depends only on the isotope (T½ = 0.693/λ), not on how much of it there is. A bigger sample has more decays per second but the same half-life._

**⚠ phy-16-032** · PHY-16.4 · M  
A radioactive source widely used in radiotherapy to destroy cancer cells with gamma rays is:  
A) Carbon-14  
**B) Cobalt-60 ✓**  
C) Uranium-238  
D) Sodium-24  
_Cobalt-60 emits strong gamma rays and is used to irradiate tumours. Carbon-14 is used in dating, and sodium-24 as a tracer for blood flow._

**⚠ phy-16-033** · PHY-16.4 · M  
To check how well a patient's thyroid gland is working, a doctor gives a small amount of a radioactive tracer. A suitable tracer is:  
**A) Iodine-131 ✓**  
B) Cobalt-60  
C) Uranium-235  
D) Carbon-14  
_The thyroid takes up iodine, so radioactive iodine-131 collects there and its radiation can be detected from outside to show how the gland is working._

**⚠ phy-16-035** · PHY-16.4 · M  
The SI unit of absorbed dose, equal to 1 joule of radiation energy absorbed per kilogram of tissue, is the:  
A) Becquerel  
**B) Gray ✓**  
C) Curie  
D) Sievert  
_Absorbed dose is measured in gray: 1 Gy = 1 J kg⁻¹. The becquerel and curie are units of activity; the sievert is used for equivalent dose, which also allows for the type of radiation._

**⚠ phy-16-038** · PHY-16.4 · D  
A radioactive tracer is injected into a patient so that an organ can be imaged by a detector outside the body. The tracer should emit:  
A) Alpha particles and have a long half-life  
B) Beta particles and have a very long half-life  
C) Alpha particles and have a short half-life  
**D) Gamma rays and have a short half-life ✓**  
_Gamma rays pass out of the body to reach the detector, whereas alpha and beta particles would be absorbed and harm tissue. A short half-life (hours) gives enough time to image while keeping the patient's dose low._
