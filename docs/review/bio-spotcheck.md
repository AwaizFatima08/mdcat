A) An injection into the pancreas  
**B) An aerosol spray inhaled into the lungs ✓**  
C) A pill swallowed with food  
D) Transplanting new bone marrow  
_Cystic fibrosis mainly damages the lungs, so the normal gene, packed in liposomes or a modified virus, was inhaled to reach the airway lining. The effect lasted only a short time because these cells are replaced._

**bio-16-086** · BIO-16.3 · M  
Human growth hormone, now made by recombinant bacteria, is normally secreted in the body by the:  
**A) Anterior pituitary gland ✓**  
B) Thyroid gland  
C) Adrenal cortex  
D) Pancreas  
_Growth hormone (somatotrophin) is secreted by the anterior lobe of the pituitary gland. The pancreas secretes insulin and glucagon, and the thyroid secretes thyroxine._

**bio-16-087** · BIO-16.3 · M  
Before genetic engineering, interferons were scarce and expensive mainly because they:  
A) Were made only by bacteria growing in hot springs  
B) Lost activity immediately after being made  
**C) Could be extracted only in tiny amounts from human blood cells ✓**  
D) Were obtained from the pancreases of pigs  
_Human interferon had to be purified from donated white blood cells, giving very small yields. Inserting interferon genes into bacteria made large-scale, cheaper production possible._

**⚠ bio-16-088** · BIO-16.3 · D  
For producing human insulin in bacteria, scientists use DNA made from insulin mRNA rather than the gene taken directly from chromosomes, because:  
**A) Bacteria cannot remove the introns present in the chromosomal gene ✓**  
B) Chromosomal DNA cannot be cut by restriction enzymes  
C) mRNA-derived DNA is double the size of the gene  
D) Bacteria cannot read DNA taken from any human cell  
_Human genes contain introns, which are removed from the mRNA in human cells. Bacteria have no machinery to remove introns, so complementary DNA made from mRNA by reverse transcriptase is used._
