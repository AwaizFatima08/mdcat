# English and Logical Reasoning addendum to WRITER_BRIEF.md

The writer brief was written for Biology; for English (`eng`) and Logical
Reasoning (`lr`), read it with these changes. Where they differ, this file wins.
Scope: `docs/sources/PMDC-Uniform-Curriculum-MDCAT-2025-Final.txt`, sections
"4. ENGLISH" and "5. LOGICAL REASONING".

## Both
- **All text must be original.** Never copy or adapt passages, poems or
  questions from books, past papers or websites. Write your own sentences.
- **Exactly one defensible answer** matters even more here: in grammar, avoid
  points where British and American usage, or formal and informal usage, differ
  (e.g. collective nouns with plural verbs, split infinitives, "data is/are").
  In reasoning, every conclusion must follow strictly from the given statements,
  never from general knowledge or opinion.
- **Letters:** the checker rejects "A and B", "(C)", "option D". Name people
  with Pakistani names (Ali, Sara, Hamza, Ayesha, Bilal, Fatima…), objects
  as P, Q, R, S, W, X, Y, Z. Letter series (LR) may use any letters, but write
  them comma-separated ("B, E, H, K, ?") and never write "A and B" in text.
- No underlined parts (the app can't show underlining). For error-spotting,
  quote the parts in the options: "The error is in 'has went'".

## English
- MDCAT English style: sentence completion with the right word or tense,
  spot the error, choose the correctly punctuated / correctly reported /
  correctly converted sentence, synonym in context, meaning of an idiom or
  figure of speech in a sentence.
- **Reading (ENG-1.x):** put a short original passage (40–90 words) in the
  stem followed by the question. Each passage is used once. Stems may be
  longer than the 45-word guide for these only.
- Quote sentences in plain double quotes. Keep vocabulary at FSc level.
- `d`: E = one obvious rule; M = a usual MDCAT trap; D = two rules interacting
  or a subtle shade of meaning — still with one clearly right answer.

## Logical Reasoning
- Question types to use, by unit: critical thinking (strong/weak argument,
  assumption, which statement must be true), series (number, letter, mixed;
  arithmetic, geometric, alternating, squares/cubes), deductions (syllogisms
  "All P are Q…", orderings, blood relations, direction sense, seating),
  logical problems (multi-clue puzzles stated fully in the stem),
  course of action (statement + proposed actions: which follow), cause and
  effect (two events: which is the cause, are they independent effects of a
  common cause).
- For course-of-action and cause-and-effect, follow the standard MDCAT/
  aptitude-test conventions and state the options in full words, e.g.
  "Only action I follows", "Both I and II follow", "Neither follows";
  "Statement I is the cause and II its effect", "Both are effects of a common
  cause". Use Roman numerals I, II, III for statements — never letters.
- Everything needed must be in the stem; puzzles must have exactly one
  solution — solve each one fully twice before keeping it.
- `x` gives the rule or the key step of the deduction in one or two lines.
